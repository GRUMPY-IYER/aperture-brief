Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Virtual cell

A computational model trained on enormous amounts of biological data that aims to simulate how a real cell would behave — the long-term bet behind Meta, Google and Isomorphic Labs pouring hundreds of millions into Biohub's data-collection effort.

Public-private research partnership

An arrangement where government funding and industry money jointly steer a research agenda, as with the Genesis Mission — useful for moving fast, but it means the companies setting direction aren't accountable to anyone outside the deal.

Science

Phys.org

Nobel Prize in Chemistry Awarded for Solving a Century-Old Mystery of Molecular Handedness

Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai shared the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for discovering non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric synthesis — reactions showing how a slight imbalance between left- and right-handed molecules can snowball into near-total one-handedness. The finding bears on one of chemistry's oldest puzzles: why life settled on one molecular handedness instead of a mix.

Phys.org

Nobel Prize in Physics Honors the Hunt for Ghostly Neutrinos From Space

Francis Halzen won the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics for building IceCube, a telescope frozen into a cubic kilometer of Antarctic ice that detects high-energy neutrinos arriving from deep space. The particles pass through almost everything undisturbed, which is exactly what makes the rare ones IceCube catches such precise messengers from violent cosmic events light-years away.

ScienceDaily

The Protein That Glues Your Cells Together Has a Second Job: Eating the Dead Ones

University of Liverpool researchers found that E-cadherin, the protein that holds epithelial cells to each other, also lets those same cells engulf dying neighbors without breaking the protective barrier they form. Epithelial cells aren't specialized immune cells, so the discovery adds a quiet, everyday cleanup crew to tissue that was thought to rely entirely on outside help.

The Life-Saving Knowledge That's Already Sitting in Plain Sight

Nature editor Helen Pearson argues that science's unglamorous work — collating and synthesizing evidence that already exists — saves more lives than the chase for new studies, yet gets a fraction of the funding and prestige. Her case is a quiet rebuttal to a research culture built almost entirely around novelty.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Cognitive offloading

Letting a tool do a piece of mental work you'd otherwise do yourself — useful in the moment, but the underlying skill can atrophy if you never have to exercise it.

Evidence synthesis

The work of systematically pulling together everything already known on a question, rather than running a new study — less glamorous than discovery, but often more useful for deciding what's actually true.
