Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
AI benchmark

A benchmark is a standardized test — a fixed set of coding problems, exam questions or tasks — used to score AI models against each other. Vendors choose which benchmarks to publicize, so 'our model wins' headlines often reflect the test chosen as much as real-world ability; independent, apples-to-apples evals are rarer and usually tell a messier story.

Human-in-the-loop moderation

Human-in-the-loop moderation means a person, not just an algorithm, reviews what an AI system produces or receives — commonly to rate quality, catch abuse or label training data. It's why outputs can feel 'safe' at scale: real people are reading a sample of the raw material, which raises its own privacy and working-conditions questions.

Philosophy

Aeon

Our Crisis of Public Knowledge Is a Crisis of Truthfulness, Not Just Truth

Writing in Aeon, Richard Reeves argues that the public's crisis of trust is really a crisis of truthfulness, not truth itself — people lie and make honest mistakes for very different reasons, but modern information systems reward both equally. Rebuilding trust, he argues, means designing institutions that reward the sincere effort to get things right, not just the correct answer after the fact.

Essentia Foundation

Pushed to Its Conclusion, Western Metaphysics Points to Idealism, Philosopher Argues

Philosopher Peter Jones argues that Western metaphysics has failed to settle its biggest questions not because they are unanswerable, but because its method of eliminating hypotheses one by one was never pushed to its conclusion. Push it all the way, he writes, and what survives is a form of nondual idealism — the view that mind, not matter, is fundamental.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Truth vs. truthfulness

Truth is a property of a statement — it matches the facts or it doesn't. Truthfulness is a property of a person — the sincere effort to say what you believe is true, even if you turn out to be wrong. Philosophers increasingly argue that public trust depends on rewarding the second even when the first briefly fails.

Idealism

In philosophy, idealism is the view that reality is fundamentally mental — that mind or consciousness is the basic stuff of the universe, and the physical world we perceive is somehow derived from it. It's the mirror image of materialism, which holds that matter is basic and mind emerges from it.
