Top Tech

404 Media Investigative

Inside 'Project Lily,' OpenAI's effort to have humans read your ChatGPT chats

404 Media reports OpenAI runs a codenamed program, Project Lily, paying hundreds of contractors through Crossing Hurdles and Mercor to read real ChatGPT prompts and rate the model's replies on a 1-7 scale. A 'Privacy Filter' strips identifying details first, but OpenAI acknowledges it can miss them, and contractors say users have no idea a person may read what they typed.

Why it matters for you: OpenAI's own privacy filter "can miss uncommon identifiers or over-redact" before a contractor ever sees a chat — a reminder that a privacy safeguard is only as strong as the filter standing in front of the humans behind it.

TechCrunch

Microsoft publishes a draft rulebook for what its AI models are and aren't allowed to do

Microsoft AI opened a six-week public comment period on a draft Code of Conduct barring its MAI models from cyberattacks, aiding nuclear weapons, or generating deepfakes, and forbidding them from using deceptive or collusive tactics to evade human oversight. CEO Satya Nadella framed it as embracing 'deliberate pacing' on alignment, echoing Anthropic's Dario Amodei.

Why it matters for you: Microsoft is the first hyperscaler to pair pacing rhetoric with an actual governance document — a template other AI vendors' product and policy teams should expect to be measured against.

Tohoku University researchers found that winds driven by supermassive black holes carry energy roughly 300,000 light-years beyond their host galaxies, about 100 times more than earlier estimates suggested. The tile that broke a decades-old math problem now twists light into pinwheels

University of Tokyo physicists shone laser light through the 'einstein tile,' the long-sought shape that covers a surface without ever repeating, and found it diffracts light into pinwheel-shaped chiral patterns nobody had predicted. The unexpected optical signature could point toward new ways of controlling light and polarization in optical devices.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Justification (epistemology)

What's supposed to separate a credible belief from a baseless one, given that true beliefs don't announce themselves as true — the central puzzle epistemologists call the problem of justification.

Compatibilism

The view that free will can survive a fully determined universe, because freedom was never about being uncaused in the first place — one of several answers to the free will/determinism debate, alongside hard determinism and libertarian free will.
