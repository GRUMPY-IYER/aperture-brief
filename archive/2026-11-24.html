Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Agentic AI

An AI 'agent' isn't just a chatbot answering questions — it's a system that takes multi-step actions on its own: deciding what to do next, running tools, and checking its own work. The ARTEX hacking tool wires that same pattern to autonomous intrusion agents that handle reconnaissance, exploitation and data theft end to end, which is why security researchers call it so dangerous even after its creator pulled it.

Zero-trust architecture

Zero-trust security assumes no device, user or network location is automatically safe — every request has to prove itself every time, rather than being trusted just because it's 'inside the firewall.' Zscaler built its business on this model; pairing it with CrowdStrike's device-level protection is a bet that endpoint security and zero-trust network access are converging into one product category rather than staying separate markets.

Philosophy

Aeon

Believing and Thinking

Philosophers Neil Van Leeuwen and Jacob Eisler argue church-state separation rests on a real psychological split: 'religious credence,' held voluntarily and shielded from evidence, versus ordinary factual belief, which is involuntary and answers to evidence. The distinction, they say, explains why governments shouldn't impose doctrine while believers still reason evidentially elsewhere.

Aeon

Is Everything Conscious?

Philosopher Sam Kimpton-Nye traces an argument for panpsychism built on the principle of sufficient reason — the idea that everything needs an explanation. If fundamental physical properties must ultimately be explained by something, he argues via thinkers like Hedda Hassel Mørch, that something may have to be consciousness, all the way down.

Aeon

Reason Is More Than a Tool

Philosopher Sasha Mudd warns that defining intelligence as pure optimisation — fine for judging a chess engine — quietly imports a machine model into how we think about human minds. Drawing on Kant against Hume, she argues reason's real job is choosing which ends are worth pursuing, a capacity AI debates risk writing out.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Religious credence

Ordinary belief ('it's raining') tracks evidence and governs your behavior everywhere. 'Religious credence' is a different kind of commitment — one held more voluntarily, largely walled off from counter-evidence and from contexts outside worship. Today's church-state essay uses that split to explain why a government can stay neutral on doctrine while a believer still reasons normally about everything else.

Panpsychism

The idea that consciousness isn't something that switches on only inside brains, but is a basic ingredient of physical reality all the way down, present in some minimal form even in particles. Today's essay gets there through the 'principle of sufficient reason': if every fundamental fact needs an explanation, and bare physical properties can't explain themselves, consciousness may be the only candidate left that can.
