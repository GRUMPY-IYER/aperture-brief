TechCrunch

Apple unveils its first foldable phone, the iPhone Duo

Apple's 'Surprise and Shine' event introduced the iPhone Duo, a book-style foldable with a 5.4-inch outer display and 7.6-inch inner screen, running on a new in-house C2 modem that further reduces Apple's reliance on Qualcomm. It arrives October 23 at $1,999, alongside a refreshed iPhone 18 Pro line, Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4, and AirPods 5.

Techmeme

US intelligence agencies accuse six Chinese AI firms of 'industrial-scale' distillation of American models

A joint NSA, CISA and FBI advisory says DeepSeek, Alibaba, Moonshot AI, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI pulled billions of tokens from Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's GPT, Google's Gemini and xAI's Grok to train their own models, using fraudulent accounts and proxy 'transfer stations' to evade detection. Rather than blocking flagged accounts outright, the agencies recommend providers quietly degrade responses to suspected distillation traffic.

Why it matters for you: When an intelligence advisory can name your product's outputs as another company's training data, abuse detection and terms-of-service enforcement stop being a policy footnote and become a security control regulators will expect you to have.

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Foldable hinge engineering

The mechanical and software work needed to make a screen bend thousands of times without a visible crease or a weak point. Apple's iPhone Duo uses a custom liquid-metal hinge and its own C2 modem, built in-house rather than bought from suppliers.

Model distillation

Training a smaller AI model by having it learn from a larger one's outputs — legitimate when done with permission, but a terms-of-service violation when done by scraping a rival's responses at scale, as US agencies allege several Chinese firms have done.

Philosophy

Big Think

Why 'hard choices' aren't about finding the objectively better option

Philosopher Ruth Chang argues that genuinely hard choices aren't cases where one option is secretly better and we just can't see it, but cases where options are 'on a par' — comparable without being equal. She suggests the way through is introspective: asking which option you could commit to and stand behind, rather than searching for a tiebreaker that doesn't exist.

IAI News

We don't have different perspectives on reality — we inhabit different realities

Sociologist Steve Fuller argues that treating disagreement as a matter of differing 'perspectives' on one shared reality undersells how differently people's frameworks actually carve up the world. Drawing on ideas from quantum mechanics and epistemology, he suggests some disagreements are less about interpretation than about which reality someone is effectively operating in.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Hard choices / parity

Philosopher Ruth Chang's term for options that are comparable in value without being equal or one being strictly better — the reasoning problem behind decisions like choosing a career, where standard comparison breaks down.

Perspectivism

The idea that what counts as true or real depends on a particular standpoint or framework, rather than there being one neutral view from nowhere. Steve Fuller pushes this further, arguing people aren't just seeing reality differently but inhabiting different ones.
