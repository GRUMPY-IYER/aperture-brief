More Than 500 U.S. Citizens Have Been Detained by Immigration Agents, ProPublica Finds

ProPublica's ongoing tracker has identified 506 U.S. citizens detained by immigration agents during the Trump administration, including at least 70 children and roughly three dozen people held for more than a day without access to a lawyer or family. The reporters say the true number is almost certainly higher, since the count includes only cases they could individually verify.

ProPublica Investigative

The FBI's Anti-Corruption Squad Was Circling Susan Collins — Until Trump Got in the Way

ProPublica reports the FBI had gathered enough evidence by late 2024 to open a sweeping bribery probe touching Senator Susan Collins and other lawmakers, tied to a defense contractor's illegal campaign contributions — before the incoming administration's cuts to the bureau's anti-corruption unit shut it down. Collins's campaign calls the allegations baseless, and the dispute spilled into a Maine Senate debate this week.

Top Tech

TechCrunch

Google Brings Agentic AI to Gemini, Starting With Businesses

Google unveiled a unified Gemini agent that can act on a user's behalf across tasks, not just answer questions, launching first for enterprise customers with its own Workspace account and email address. Users can also hand the agent off to a different underlying model, including Anthropic's Claude, rather than being locked into Google's own.

Why it matters for you: An agent that gets its own company email address and picks its own model is a preview of how "AI seat" procurement will work — budget for an identity and access review, not just an API key.

TechCrunch

Anthropic Changes Usage Policy to Ban Model Abuse and Election Interference

Anthropic's revised usage policy adds explicit bans on using Claude to deceive voters or disrupt elections, alongside new rules against weapons development and surveillance misuse, plus a narrower rule against sustained, purposeless cruelty toward the model itself. The company says ordinary frustration and criticism of its products remain fine.

Why it matters for you: Vendor usage policies are becoming a compliance surface in their own right — know what your product is and isn't allowed to do under each model provider's terms before you ship, not after.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Agentic AI

An AI system that doesn't just answer questions but takes actions on a user's behalf — booking, emailing, filing — the shift Google's enterprise Gemini launch and a wave of competitors are now racing toward.

Usage policy

The rules an AI company sets for what its models may be used for, separate from what they're technically capable of — increasingly a compliance document worth reading as carefully as a terms-of-service agreement.

Science

Phys.org

Nearly 10,000 Mapped Reactions Reveal Overlooked Steps in CO2-to-Fuel Conversion

Indian Institute of Science researchers built a data-driven model of nearly 9,400 individual reactions in CO2 hydrogenation on a copper catalyst, a scale previous studies couldn't afford to simulate directly. The expanded model raised predicted CO2 conversion roughly 40-fold and correctly flagged methanol as the dominant product, pointing to catalyst designs full quantum modeling alone would have missed.

ScienceDaily

Chang'e-6 Lunar Soil Contains a Surprising Magnetic Time Capsule

Chinese researchers studying Chang'e-6 lunar soil identified a previously unknown magnetic mineral, a form of metallic iron called γ-Fe, locked inside tiny impact-glass particles. The nanoscale grains may act as a fossil record of the Moon's long-vanished magnetic field, giving scientists a new way to reconstruct how it changed over time.

Phys.org

Blue-Light Labeling Uncovers Unexpected Protein Partners of Folded DNA Structures

Japanese researchers attached a light-triggered photocatalyst directly to a G-quadruplex, a four-stranded DNA knot, letting blue light tag only the proteins actually touching it in that instant. The method caught hexokinase-1, an energy-metabolism enzyme, as a previously unrecognized binding partner — a catch that cruder capture methods had been missing for years.

Philosophy

IAI News

China's Radical Philosophy of Action Dissolves the Free Will Problem

Philosopher Brian Bruya argues that the Western free-will debate rests on a split between determined nature and free, accountable humans that classical Chinese thought never made. Drawing on the Zhuangzi's picture of spontaneous, skilled action without a reflective self weighing options, he suggests it fits recent cognitive science better than anything Western compatibilism has produced.

Aeon

The Self Is Not Singular but a Fluid Network of Identities

Rejecting theories that reduce the self to a body, a memory stream or a single social role, this essay argues personal identity is constituted by a shifting web of relationships, roles and group memberships. On this "network self" view, a person's identity can change as thoroughly as their relationships do, without ceasing to be the same person.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Free will problem

The puzzle of how humans can be genuinely responsible for their actions if everything we do is caused by prior events in a deterministic universe — a framing some philosophers argue is itself a peculiarly Western invention.

Network self

A view of personal identity that locates the self not in the body or memory alone but in a person's whole web of relationships and social roles — which can shift over a lifetime without the person ceasing to be themselves.
