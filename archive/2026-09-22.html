Techmeme

Apple Escalates Trade-Secrets Fight With OpenAI Over Hardware Plans

Apple asked the court overseeing its trade-secrets suit against OpenAI to let its own experts review forensic images from a former employee's MacBook, while seeking OpenAI hardware R&D documents of its own. Apple's lawyers say the laptop shows a confidential power-converter circuit file being used in simulations and evidence of instructions to destroy data; OpenAI calls the dispute a result of Apple's own flawed offboarding process.

Why it matters for you: A discovery fight over what counts as 'destroying evidence' during an employee's move to a competitor is worth watching for how it shapes offboarding policy anywhere trade secrets and departing talent overlap.

Techmeme

Amazon and Walmart's Drone Deliveries Are Overwhelming a Texas Suburb

Amazon and Walmart drones are flying more than a hundred deliveries a day over Richardson, Texas, after a no-fly zone around a hospital funneled most traffic over one neighborhood; residents describe the noise as 'a loud swarm of bees.' The city council is weighing whether it can legally claw back Amazon's zoning after the FAA signed off on the routes in July.

Why it matters for you: A backlash born from where a product routes its volume, not from what was built, is easy to miss in a launch plan — worth war-gaming neighborhood-level externalities before scaling any physical delivery product.

Traffic to News Sites Is Collapsing as AI Interfaces Replace Search and Social

Traffic to US news sites is falling sharply as tech platforms shift users toward AI interfaces that answer questions directly instead of linking out, forcing publishers into faster pivots, layoffs and consolidation. Why it matters for you: If a product depends on organic search or social referral traffic, the same platform shift now eating publishers' audiences is worth stress-testing against your own acquisition funnel before the numbers move.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Trade-secret discovery

In a lawsuit over stolen trade secrets, 'discovery' is the phase where each side can demand the other's internal files and devices — here, a former employee's laptop — to prove or disprove that confidential material was taken and used.

Dark social

Traffic a website can't trace to its source because it arrived through a private channel — a text message, an email, a DM — rather than a clickable public link, making it one of the few referral paths an AI chatbot can't intercept.

A Cheap New Process Turns Plastic Waste Into Gasoline and Diesel

Oak Ridge National Laboratory chemists found that inexpensive aluminum-based molten salts can break long polyethylene molecules — the plastic in shopping bags and cutting boards — into smaller hydrocarbons under relatively mild conditions, yielding about 60% gasoline. ScienceDaily

An Underground Experiment Rules Out One Theory of What Kills Schrödinger's Cat

Physicists beneath Italy's Gran Sasso mountain spent 62 days hunting for a faint radiation signal that would confirm a decades-old theory: that ripples in spacetime caused by gravity gradually destroy quantum superpositions, which is why cats are never observed both alive and dead. They found no such signal, ruling out gravity as the explanation and narrowing the search for what actually forces a quantum system into one definite state.

Noema

AI Is Coming for the Meritocracy

Branko Milanović and Nils Gilman revisit political theorist James Burnham's 1941 claim that capitalism was giving way to 'managerialism' — control passing not to owners but to the people who know how to run things — and argue AI is now hollowing out the managerial and professional jobs that same shift once created. It's a political-philosophy argument that the ladder of upward mobility itself, not just individual jobs, is what AI puts at risk.

Noema

Why AI Writing Feels Impoverished, Even When It's Fluent

Writer Afra Wang argues that great writing has never come from systems of reward and punishment, tracing a line from Baudelaire's France through the 'Simplified Chinese Internet' to the reward-modeling that shapes how large language models write today. Her case: the flattening readers sense in AI prose isn't a bug to be fine-tuned away but a structural consequence of optimizing language for approval.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Managerialism

James Burnham's 1941 theory that capitalism wasn't being replaced by socialism but by a system where control of production passes to professional managers rather than owners or workers — a lens some now apply to argue AI is displacing the managers in turn.

Model collapse

The tendency of an AI system trained partly on its own past output, or optimized to please raters, to gradually lose the variety and edge of the human writing it started from — producing text that is fluent but flatter with each generation.
