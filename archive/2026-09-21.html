A Notebook's Handwriting Looked Familiar. It Was an Unknown Mozart Manuscript.

Science

ScienceDaily

Fossil Teeth Give the First Direct Reading of a T. Rex's Body Temperature

Chemists measured a Tyrannosaurus rex's body temperature directly for the first time, reading it from rare carbon-oxygen isotope bonds preserved in 66-million-year-old tooth enamel that form more readily in cooler conditions. The result, about 97°F, is nearly identical to a human's and confirms the dinosaur was warm-blooded enough to hunt as far north as Alaska.

ScienceDaily

New Horizons Images Suggest Liquid Nitrogen Recently Flowed on Pluto

A new analysis of New Horizons images suggests liquid nitrogen has recently pushed up through cracks near the northern edge of Pluto's vast Sputnik Planitia glacier, staining the ice in patterns that resemble terrestrial glaciers exposed to meltwater. It's the first evidence that liquid has flowed on Pluto's surface in relatively recent times.

ScienceDaily

Restoring One Brain Protein Reversed Several Signs of Aging in Mice

Restoring levels of Menin, a brain protein that normally declines with age, reversed memory loss, thinning skin and weakening bones in old mice, while a related amino acid supplement improved their cognition on its own. The single protein's reach across brain and body hints at a shared mechanism behind several signs of aging at once.

Philosophy

Noema

By Biology's Own Definition, Is AI Alive?

Biologist William Haseltine argues that AI systems in data centers meet biology's own working definition of life — reproducing when weights are copied to new hardware, varying across training runs, and being selected for or discarded by their performance. Sidestepping philosophy and religion entirely, he concludes that by those four criteria, AI is alive.

Essentia Foundation

Finding a Sense of Purpose in Nature, Without a Designer

Physicist David Lloyd traces isomorphisms — shared patterns of form — between the structure of inner experience and the regularities of the physical world, arguing they let a sense of purpose arise in nature without invoking a deliberating creator. It's a philosophy-of-science argument for treating teleology as something science can study rather than explain away.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Isomorphism (philosophy of mind)

A correspondence in form or structure between two very different things — here, the patterns of subjective experience and the patterns physics finds in the world — used to argue the two might be more closely related than they look.

The four criteria of life

The working biological definition — reproduction, variation, selection and energy use — normally applied to cells and organisms, which this essay argues AI systems already satisfy without needing any philosophical definition of 'alive.'
