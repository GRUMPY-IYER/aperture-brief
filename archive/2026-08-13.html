Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Series C / valuation

When you read "$143M Series C at a $1.5B valuation" (like CodeRabbit today), it means this is the startup's third major funding round, and investors are collectively betting the whole company is worth $1.5 billion, even though it just raised a much smaller slice of that. The valuation isn't a bank appraisal; it's the price new investors agreed to pay for their slice, extrapolated to the whole company. A jump from a $60M Series B to a $1.5B valuation in under a year signals investors think the company's growth rate, not just its current size, justifies the bigger number.

Agentic tooling

It used to be that a human wrote code and other humans reviewed it before it went live. Now AI tools can write the code, review it, and even decide whether it's safe to ship, with a human stepping in only for risky calls. "Agentic" just means the AI isn't only answering questions, it's taking actions on its own. Businesses like CodeRabbit and Blacksmith exist because that shift created a new problem: when both humans and AI agents generate work at once, someone (or something) needs to referee it.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Non-Duality (Advaita)

Most of us assume there's a "me" in here looking out at a world of separate "other" things. Advaita Vedanta, literally "not-two," says that split is a kind of illusion: underneath the apparent multiplicity is one undivided awareness, and what you call "yourself" isn't a fragment of it but that awareness itself, temporarily looking out through a particular body and mind. "Tat Tvam Asi," Thou art That, is the tradition's shorthand for this.

Analytic Idealism

Physicalism assumes matter is fundamental and consciousness somehow gets produced by arranging enough of it correctly, which is why nobody can explain how mere matter starts "feeling like something" from the inside. Analytic idealism flips the order: consciousness is the one fundamental thing, and what we call matter is how conscious processes appear from the outside, the way a brain scan is an outside view of someone's inner thoughts.
