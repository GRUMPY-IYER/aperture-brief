Trump and Tech CEOs Rebrand AI as 'Super Intelligence,' Sign Voluntary Safety Accord

President Trump signed an executive order directing federal agencies to refer to AI as 'Super Intelligence,' while the chiefs of Google, Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, Nvidia, Tesla and SpaceX signed a voluntary White House accord promising layered internal monitoring and independent audits. Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
"Super Intelligence" (rebrand)

In September 2026 the Trump administration began requiring federal agencies to call AI 'Super Intelligence' instead, while the underlying legal definition of artificial intelligence stays the same. Renaming a technology doesn't change what it does — watch whether the substance (safety testing, audits, liability) changes along with the label, or only the label does.

Agentic red-teaming

Traditional penetration testing uses a small team to probe a system once or twice a year. 'Agent swarm' security tools instead run many autonomous AI agents continuously, each hunting for and chaining together vulnerabilities the way a real attacker would — trading a periodic snapshot for always-on coverage, at the cost of trusting an AI agent with deep system access.

A Diet Rich in Berries and Greens May Slow Brain Aging by Years

A 12-year Framingham Heart Study analysis found that adults who more closely followed the MIND diet — a Mediterranean-DASH hybrid emphasizing berries, leafy greens and poultry — showed about 20% less grey-matter shrinkage per three-point increase in diet score, equivalent to roughly 2.5 years of delayed brain aging. Good Science Requires More Than Just Evidence

Philosopher David Kyle Johnson argues that evidence alone has never been enough to judge a scientific theory — qualities like parsimony and explanatory scope have always done real work in deciding between theories that fit the same data. The piece is a reminder that 'follow the evidence' understates how much judgment science actually requires.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Theoretical virtues

Beyond fitting the evidence, philosophers of science judge theories by qualities like parsimony (fewer assumptions), scope (explaining more), and fruitfulness (predicting new findings) — the 'theoretical virtues' that help choose between rival theories that explain the same data equally well.

Optimization (in philosophy of mind)

To optimize is to maximize or minimize some single measurable quantity — a chess engine optimizes for winning positions, an ad algorithm for clicks. Defining intelligence as optimization is useful for building AI, but critics note it says nothing about whether the goal being optimized for is itself a good one.
