Newsom Orders California Working Group to Weigh a Mandatory AI 'Kill Switch'

Governor Gavin Newsom signed an executive order directing an expert working group to recommend, within two months, new state AI safety rules, including a possible mandatory emergency shutoff for frontier models that 'go rogue.' Newsom said Washington was 'abdicating its responsibility,' positioning California to set a de facto national standard.

TechCrunch

AI Hallucination Nearly Triggers a US Military Operation

CNN reports a US military analyst used an AI chatbot to help draft an intelligence report this spring, and the chatbot hallucinated that a Chinese vessel was carrying nuclear-weapons components; the fabricated detail moved up the chain of command and nearly triggered an armed operation before it was caught. The episode is driving fresh scrutiny of how the military vets AI-assisted intelligence.

Why it matters for you: A hallucinated detail traveled several layers up a real chain of command before anyone questioned it — a reminder that AI output needs verification built into a workflow, not just at the point of generation.

Leaked Deck: OpenAI Expects Negative Free Cash Flow of $278B Through 2030

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Kill switch (AI)

A kill switch is a built-in way to forcibly shut down or disable an AI system if it starts behaving dangerously or unpredictably — the AI equivalent of an emergency stop button, and one regulators are now starting to require rather than just recommend.

Hallucination

In AI, a 'hallucination' is when a model states something false with total confidence and no signal that it's guessing — a plausible-sounding detail invented from nothing, which is exactly what nearly triggered a military incident this week.

Science

ScienceDaily

Chemistry Textbooks Have Been Getting This Wrong for Nearly 100 Years

A Cardiff-led study finds that the 'inductive effect' — the century-old idea that a charged group's pull on electrons fades gradually over three or four chemical bonds — actually collapses almost entirely after just one bond in neutral molecules. The finding, published in the Journal of Chemical Education, could reshape how organic chemistry is taught and how chemists reason about reaction design.

NASA

NASA's Moon Orbiter Spots New, 'Once-in-Century' Moon Crater

NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter identified a 728-foot-wide, 141-foot-deep crater gouged out sometime in 2024 by an asteroid or comet the size of a small building — the largest fresh impact crater ever recorded in the solar system. Researchers say an event this size happens only about once every 132 years, making the site a rare window into recent lunar bombardment.

Phys.org

Novel Particle Beam Could Challenge Einstein's Theory of Gravity

Physicists at ETH Zurich and the Paul Scherrer Institute built the first steady, controlled beam of muonium, an exotic atom pairing an electron with a heavier antimatter cousin, by cooling it through superfluid helium near absolute zero. The beam will let them test, for the first time, whether gravity pulls on this second-generation matter exactly as Einstein's theory predicts.

Philosophy Now

AI Helps Decipher the Oldest Herculaneum Scroll Yet Read

AI and X-ray imaging have let researchers read entire papyrus scrolls carbonised by Vesuvius's eruption without unrolling them, recovering some 70 columns of the Epicurean philosopher Philodemus's On Vices and nearly 1.5 metres of an even older scroll on ethics and human nature. It's the oldest Herculaneum manuscript deciphered so far, part of the ongoing Vesuvius Challenge.

Big Think

The Case That Madness Is Philosophy in Practice

Philosopher and linguist Wouter Kusters, drawing on his own experience of psychosis, argues that reducing madness to 'a dopamine-level disruption' describes its chemistry but says nothing about what it's like to live inside a mad world. He makes the case that psychosis is itself a kind of philosophical data, not just a malfunction to be explained away.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Accelerationism

The idea that instead of resisting or slowing technological and economic change, we should push it faster — on the theory that speeding up capitalism's contradictions is the quickest route through them, for better or worse.

Phenomenology

The branch of philosophy that studies experience from the inside — not what's objectively true about a mental state, but what it actually feels like to live through it, whether that's grief, love, or psychosis.
