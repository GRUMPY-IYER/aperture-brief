The Trump Administration Quietly Subpoenaed a National Teachers Union Over Pro-Palestine Student Activists

A federal grand jury subpoena, obtained exclusively by The Intercept, seeks the American Federation of Teachers' membership rolls and meeting minutes going back to 2023, part of a probe into University of Michigan protesters now facing felony charges. The union's campus local is fighting to quash a similar subpoena, calling it an attempt to criminalize labor organizing alongside student dissent.

The Intercept

A New HUD Rule Would Force Mixed-Status Families to Choose Between Separation and Eviction

A final HUD rule taking effect in mid-December will cut off federal housing assistance to households where any member is undocumented, an estimated 80,000 people including 37,000 children. U.S. citizens in those households face new verification burdens too, and HUD's own analysis puts the added cost at roughly $300 million even as the agency does less on homelessness broadly.

Former Williams colleagues staff his office; one kept his old job while serving as his chief of staff.

Two Jihadist Factions Are Killing Each Other in Burkina Faso, and No One's Sure That's Good News

Australian Debutant Mahli Beardman Takes 6/24 to Rout England in Perth ODI

Mahli Beardman, 21, took 6/24 on his ODI debut as England collapsed to 131 all out in the first ODI in Perth, dismissing captain Harry Brook and Joe Root inside the first 14 overs. Nvidia's Jensen Huang Resigns as CEO After Affair Scandal, Erasing $855 Billion in Market Value

Nvidia's board accepted Huang's resignation days after he admitted to an affair with an alleged Chinese spy. CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO; shares have fallen nearly 19% since Friday, and the company reports earnings Tuesday without him for the first time in its history.

Why it matters for you: A company this large had no visible succession plan for a 33-year founder-CEO — a reminder that even product org charts need a named backup two levels deep, not just at the top.

AI agent

Science

Phys.org

A Reusable 'Molecular Fabric' Could End One of Chemistry's Most Energy-Hungry Jobs

A self-assembling, woven fabric built from interlocking molecular networks pulled about 98% of the benzene out of a benzene-cyclohexane mixture, chemicals nearly impossible to separate by distillation because they boil at almost the same temperature. The fabric can be heated and reused, aimed at one of industry's biggest energy sinks.

ScienceDaily

Perseverance Finds Organic Carbon Just Beneath the Surface of an Ancient Mars Lakebed

NASA's rover detected complex organic carbon a few microns deep in Jezero Crater mudstone, the shallowest such find yet on Mars. It doesn't prove ancient life, since non-biological chemistry can also produce organics, but it shows carbon-based material was widespread across the planet's ancient surface.

ScienceDaily

A Magnetism Breakthrough Could Let AI Chips Run on Far Less Power

Brown University engineers grew ultrathin films of a 'multiferroic' material that keep their electrical and magnetic properties at a few atoms thick, avoiding a defect that normally kills such effects when shrunk. The films unexpectedly also showed a fast, compact magnetic state that could underpin low-power memory and logic chips.

Philosophy

Aeon

A Good Sceptic Has More Than Doubt and Less Than Certainty

Philosopher Massimo Pigliucci revives Cicero's Academic Scepticism: beliefs strong enough to act on but never certain, always open to revision. He contrasts this with climate-denying 'scepticism' on one side and certainty-demanding scientism on the other, and offers it as a working method for science, politics and daily life.

Aeon

Habermas's Theory of Truth Meant He Changed His Mind

Emilie Prattico traces how Jürgen Habermas abandoned his own earlier claim that truth is just what people would agree to under fair conditions, once he recognized facts answer to the world while moral claims answer only to discourse, a distinction she argues matters more as social media fragments public debate.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Probabilism

Cicero's idea that you can hold a belief strongly enough to act on it without claiming certainty, and should drop it the moment better evidence shows up.

Deliberative democracy

The idea that political decisions are legitimate when they come from open, reasoned debate among citizens, not just from counting votes.
