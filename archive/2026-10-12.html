US Politics

Mother Jones Investigative

ICE Is Separating Migrant Children From Parents and Calling Them 'Unaccompanied,' Mother Jones Finds

Mother Jones reports that immigration agents are splitting children from their parents in the US interior and then classifying them as "unaccompanied minors" bound for the refugee resettlement system, rather than for reunification. The designation routes the children into foster care and makes it harder for parents, often detained separately, to get them back.

NPR Investigative

ICE Has Quietly Been Using Warehouses as Detention Centers, NPR Finds

NPR reports that a Roxbury, New Jersey warehouse has held immigration detainees all summer, according to neighbors, even though a court order bars DHS from converting that site pending an environmental review. DHS bought roughly a billion dollars' worth of warehouses earlier this year for detention use, a plan it largely shelved after lawsuits and local backlash.

Top Tech

Axios

AI Executives Are Privately Gaming Out the 'Day After' a Catastrophic AI Event

Axios reports that top executives at Anthropic, OpenAI and other AI companies are privately rehearsing how the public and politicians might react to a catastrophic AI event, most likely a major cyberattack, with some predicting such an event within six to twelve months. The exercises amount to red-teaming the industry's own crisis response, not just its models.

Why it matters for you: If your own org has no equivalent incident-response rehearsal for an AI-caused failure, this is a sign the frontier labs think that gap is now a real liability, not a hypothetical one.

404 Media

Lawmakers Introduce Bills to Curb Flock's License-Plate Surveillance After 404 Media's Reporting

Multiple lawmakers across party lines have introduced legislation that would sharply limit federal agencies' access to Flock's license-plate-reader network, citing 404 Media's coverage of how the system has been used. It follows Flock's own staff cuts last week amid a court ruling that one of its searches was unconstitutional.

Why it matters for you: A vendor whose product is drawing bipartisan legislation, not just a lawsuit, is a sharper procurement-risk signal than the court case alone — worth tracking if you rely on similar surveillance-adjacent infrastructure.

Science

Oregon State University

A Sulfur Bond That Splits in Sunlight Could Make Hydrogen Fuel Without Expensive Metal Catalysts

Oregon State University chemists built a light-activated metal-organic framework, BVR-19, whose L-cystine building blocks contain a sulfur-to-sulfur bond that breaks apart under sunlight to generate hydrogen from water. Because the reactive chemistry lives in the sulfur-containing organic linkers rather than in a precious-metal co-catalyst, the approach points toward a cheaper route to green hydrogen; the work, led by Kyriakos Stylianou, is published in the Journal of the American Chemical Society.

ScienceDaily

Webb Finds Scars of Mars-Sized Planets Smashing Together Around Young Stars

NASA's James Webb Space Telescope spotted 21 rare "extreme debris disks" around young stars, roughly a third of them silica-rich in a way that points to violent collisions between Mars-sized bodies rather than gentler dust accumulation. Only about 1% of young stars show this phase, and the findings, published October 1 in The Astrophysical Journal, offer a rare look at the kind of giant impact thought to have formed our own Moon.

Philosophy

IAI News

A New Study Suggests Heavy LLM Use Is Reducing Brain Function

Drawing on an MIT study of students who wrote essays with ChatGPT's help, Minh-Hoang Nguyen argues that outsourcing the struggle of thinking to a language model doesn't just save time, it atrophies the capacity for independent thought. His remedy isn't abstaining from AI but deliberately preserving room to be stupid and slow, since that discomfort is where the thinking actually happens.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Cognitive offloading

Handing a mental task to an external tool rather than doing it yourself — useful for remembering phone numbers, Nguyen argues, but corrosive for the kind of struggle that builds the capacity to think in the first place.
