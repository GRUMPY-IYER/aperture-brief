Google Backs Off Part of Its Plan to Cut Off Search for Schools That Ban AI

Aeon

How Renaissance Italy Learned to Love the Gun It Once Called Diabolical

Historian Catherine Fletcher traces how Renaissance Italians, who first condemned firearms as unchivalrous and diabolical, embraced them through militias, shooting contests and hunting — Duke Cosimo de' Medici alone owned 91 guns by 1539. Guns became tied to masculinity and civic liberty even as their criminal and political misuse grew alongside them.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Religious credence

A way of holding a religious belief that differs psychologically from an ordinary factual belief — it's voluntary, often insulated from contrary evidence, and can shift with context, as in the 'Sunday effect,' where people act on beliefs in church they set aside the rest of the week.

Civic militia

A citizen force, armed and organized locally rather than by a standing army, that Renaissance Italian city-states used both for defense and to tie gun ownership to full civic membership — a link between arms and liberty that long outlasted the period.
