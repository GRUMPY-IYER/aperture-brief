OpenAI says its new GPT-6 Astra model may represent AGI

OpenAI president Greg Brockman called GPT-6 Astra a 'generational leap' that could eventually be seen as the arrival of artificial general intelligence. It's also the first model OpenAI has classified as reaching a 'critical' cybersecurity threshold — able to find and exploit unknown vulnerabilities without step-by-step human guidance.

Why it matters for you: A vendor calling its own model AGI-adjacent is a marketing claim as much as a technical one — read the safety classification, not the press line, before planning a roadmap around it.

US and China to discuss AI-directed cyberattacks at September 24 talks

Ahead of a planned Trump-Xi summit, sources say the US will push a proposal for American and Chinese AI labs to share information and self-regulate against AI-directed cyberattacks, while China is expected to revisit US chip export controls.

Techmeme

All 20 G20 nations endorse a light-touch US framework for AI regulation

The non-binding 'Carolina Principles,' endorsed unanimously including by China and Russia, urge sector-specific rules over new regulators and close coordination with industry, reserving new regulation for genuinely novel risks rather than every new AI capability.

Why it matters for you: A global consensus for light-touch rules lowers the near-term compliance bar everywhere at once — useful now, but today's product decisions may need retrofitting once any one bloc breaks ranks.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
AGI (Artificial General Intelligence)

A hypothetical AI that matches or exceeds human ability across essentially any intellectual task, rather than excelling narrowly. No model has been independently verified to reach it, so a company's own AGI claim is a marketing and morale signal as much as a technical benchmark.

Export controls

Government restrictions on selling sensitive technology, like advanced AI chips, to specific countries — the US's main lever for slowing a rival's AI progress. China raising them at AI-safety talks signals it still sees the restrictions, not shared safety norms, as the real agenda.

Researchers created davyne, a mineral-inspired material that shifts color only in near-infrared light, invisible to the naked eye and detectable solely with a spectrometer or infrared camera. Aeon

In Zen, asking whether you have free will is the wrong question

The free will versus determinism debate assumes a fixed 'I' who either chooses freely or doesn't, this essay argues — but Zen, echoing Jung's account of the ego as a mere complex of the unconscious, questions whether that self exists at all. If there's no one to be pushed around, the question dissolves rather than resolves.

US philosophy graduates had a lower 2024 unemployment rate than computer science graduates, as AI firms recruit for expertise in ethics, reasoning and safety. As AI spreads through healthcare, law and defence, questions of fairness and accountability are pulling philosophers straight out of academia.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Ego (in Jung's sense)

For Jung, the ego isn't a fixed inner ruler but one complex among many in the unconscious — a useful but partial construct. It's the idea this week's Zen essay leans on to argue there may be no fixed 'self' to have or lack free will in the first place.

AI alignment

The work of making an AI system's goals and behavior match human intentions and values, rather than just its stated instructions. It draws directly on ethics and philosophy of mind — one reason philosophy graduates are increasingly hired into AI labs.
