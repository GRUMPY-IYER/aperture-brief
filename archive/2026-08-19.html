Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
AI "Preparedness Framework"

An internal rulebook AI companies use to test new models against specific danger levels — like whether a model could meaningfully help someone run a cyberattack — before deciding whether it's safe to release. When a model crosses a "critical" threshold, the company is supposed to pause and add safeguards, which is exactly what OpenAI just did with Astra. No regulator currently mandates this; it's a self-imposed circuit breaker.

Supervoting shares

A special class of stock where each share carries outsized voting power, often 10x or more than ordinary shares. Founders who hold onto supervoting shares can keep control of company decisions even after selling most of their economic stake to public investors — which is why Anthropic is reportedly setting this up before its IPO despite CEO Dario Amodei owning only about 2% of the company.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Analytic Idealism

The view, most associated with philosopher Bernardo Kastrup, that consciousness is the one fundamental "stuff" of reality, and everything we call physical — brains, rocks, galaxies — is really a pattern of experience appearing within a single universal mind. It's a modern, rigorously argued cousin of older non-dual traditions like Advaita Vedanta.

Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems

In 1931, mathematician Kurt Gödel proved that any sufficiently powerful system of mathematics will always contain true statements it cannot prove, and can never fully verify its own consistency from the inside. In plain terms: no matter how good the rulebook, some questions about reality will always slip through the cracks of reason alone.
