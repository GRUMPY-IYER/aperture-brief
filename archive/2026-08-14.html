Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Down-round discipline in a mega-valuation market

When you read "wanted $15B, settled for $5B" (Databricks) or a valuation jumping from $26B to a reported $40B (Cognition) in the same news cycle, it looks contradictory, but it isn't. Investors are still paying up for companies with real, fast-growing revenue (Cognition's usage is up 50% a month), while pushing back on the size of a check when a company doesn't need the extra cash. The valuation number is just the price the last dollar in agreed to pay for its slice; it doesn't mean every dollar before it was priced the same way.

Agentic autonomy ("auto mode")

Most AI coding tools have worked like an intern who asks permission before every step. "Auto mode," which Anthropic made the default for Claude Code today, flips that: the AI proceeds on its own unless an action looks irreversible or destructive, only pausing for genuinely risky calls. The bet is that constant human approval isn't actually safer, since people tend to rubber-stamp prompts they see over and over, so a well-designed autonomous system can catch more real problems than a tired human clicking "approve."

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
The Hard Problem of Consciousness

Neuroscience can now show, with real precision, which brain regions light up when you see red, feel pain, or recall a memory. What it can't yet explain is why any of that physical activity is accompanied by an inner experience at all, rather than happening "in the dark," like a thermostat processing temperature with nobody home. That gap between explaining the mechanism and explaining the feeling is what philosopher David Chalmers named the "hard problem," and it's the puzzle behind today's Christof Koch piece.

Panpsychism

If you can't explain how consciousness switches on once matter gets sufficiently complex, one alternative is to stop assuming it switches on at all. Panpsychism proposes that some very basic form of experience is a fundamental feature of matter itself, present in some minimal degree everywhere, the way mass or charge are, rather than a special extra ingredient that only appears in brains. It doesn't mean rocks are thinking; it means the building blocks of experience may go all the way down.
