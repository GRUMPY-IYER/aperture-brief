TechCrunch

Why Data Centers Have Become Big Tech's Biggest Local Political Problem

Journalist Paris Marx's new book Hyperscale argues data centers draw political backlash because their costs — strained power grids, water supplies and noise — are local and physical, while generative AI's promised benefits haven't materialized to justify the buildout. He says industry fixes like dropped NDAs and closed-loop cooling don't address the underlying scale problem.

Why it matters for you: Data-center siting now carries real political and permitting risk — any PM planning AI infrastructure should budget for power, water and community transparency as early as the roadmap, not after a local backlash.

TechCrunch

OpenAI's Chief Scientist Proposes a Shared Metric for Capping How Powerful AI Gets

Jakub Pachocki proposes 'perplexity' — a measure of how well a model predicts text — as a durable benchmark for pacing AI development, since capability tests go stale fast. OpenAI's own models have already dropped below a perplexity level he associates with transformative cybersecurity and math ability, echoing Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's call to 'pace the frontier.'

Why it matters for you: If a shared metric like this becomes an industry commitment, product roadmaps built on ever-more-capable models may need to plan around a self-imposed ceiling, not just compute and cost.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Perplexity

A measure of how well an AI model predicts the next word in a sequence — lower perplexity means better prediction. OpenAI's chief scientist wants it adopted industry-wide as a durable yardstick for AI capability, since task-specific benchmarks get swamped by training to the test within months.

Hyperscale data center

A data center built at a scale — often hundreds of thousands of servers — far beyond a typical corporate facility, built specifically to serve cloud and AI workloads. Critics argue it's this massive scale, not data centers as such, that strains local power grids and water supplies enough to trigger political backlash.

Aeon

Gödel Didn't Prove There Are Limits to What We Can Know

Alexander V Gheorghiu argues Gödel's incompleteness theorems describe the limits of one version of mathematics, not of knowledge itself — building on Michael Dummett's idea that mathematical truth rests on how we justify and extend our practices. Truth isn't a ceiling we keep failing to reach, he argues, but something that grows as we faithfully extend the rules.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Indefinite extensibility

Philosopher Michael Dummett's idea that some concepts, like 'natural number' or 'provable,' are rules for continuing rather than finished definitions — every faithful extension of the rules opens room for a further one. It's the key move in arguing that Gödel's theorems limit a particular system, not knowledge as such.

Intentional community

A household or group that chooses to live and share resources together around a common purpose, rather than by family or chance. Writer Elizabeth Oldfield argues it's one of the few practical correctives to the isolation of modern individualist life, though she admits most don't last.
