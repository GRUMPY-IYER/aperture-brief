After 150 Years of Digging at Troy, Archaeologists Finally Found Its Music

North Korean Hackers Blamed for Record $6.3 Billion Binance Breach

Blockchain forensics firms TRM Labs and Elliptic have attributed the record $6.3 billion theft from Binance's wallets to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, the same cell blamed for 2025's $1.4 billion Bybit hack. Why it matters for you: The breach traced back to one engineer's compromised laptop reaching a production service container — a reminder that your attack surface is every employee's device, not just your servers.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Free cash flow

The cash a company has left after operating expenses and capital spending — the number investors are now watching anxiously as Alphabet, Amazon and Meta all post negative figures despite surging AI revenue, because it measures whether the spending is sustainable, not just whether the business is growing.

Hardcoded credential

A password or key written directly into an app's code rather than stored in a secure backend system. Anyone who has the app can extract it, which is how a researcher turned Applebee's Android app into a password for its entire customer database.

Aeon

Why Real Intelligence Has to Be More Than Optimisation

Philosopher Sasha Mudd argues that defining intelligence as optimisation, a fair description of most AI, quietly smuggles in Hume's claim that reason only serves our existing ends. Drawing on Kant, she argues real intelligence can judge which ends are worth pursuing at all, and warns that treating 'alignment' as purely technical lets a society's actual goals, like engagement or growth, escape scrutiny entirely.

Aeon

Why We Should Heed Barbara Ward's Vision of Planetary Justice

Economist Barbara Ward, who coined the term 'sustainable development' in the 1970s, argued that prosperity, equality and the environment had to be governed together at a planetary scale, not fought over nation by nation. Historian Or Rosenboim argues her vision anticipated today's linked crises, though her Western-centric, Christian framing needed the postcolonial correction she herself moved toward late in life.

Aeon

In Praise of the Dilettante

Jay Miller defends the dilettante, someone who pursues many interests for delight rather than career or credentials, against the modern cult of specialisation, tracing the insult back to Goethe and Schiller's 1799 essay on dilettantism. He argues a life of rotating passions, like a cosmopolitan who travels between crafts without settling in any one, can be as meaningful as a life built around a single vocation.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Heteronomy

In Kant's ethics, being governed by ends imposed from outside yourself, such as a market signal or an algorithm's prediction, rather than ones you've reasoned your way to and would endorse. Today's essay on AI and intelligence borrows the term to describe what's at stake in letting optimisation define our goals for us.

Dilettante

Someone who pursues a craft or interest for pleasure rather than profession or credential — a term that curdled from a neutral Baroque-Italian label into an insult, which today's essay tries to reclaim as a form of freedom rather than a lack of seriousness.
