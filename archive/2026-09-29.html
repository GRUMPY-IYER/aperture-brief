Trump Cancels $810 Million in Funding Using a Rare, Contested 'Pocket Rescission'

The White House canceled $810 million in congressionally approved spending — most of it for refugee, health and education programs — by announcing the cuts so close to the fiscal year's end that Congress's mandatory 45-day review window expires before it can object. Government watchdogs and lawmakers in both parties call the maneuver, a 'pocket rescission,' illegal; it is the second year running Trump has used it to bypass Congress on spending.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Pocket rescission

A budget maneuver where a president formally proposes canceling already-approved spending so close to the fiscal year's end that Congress's required 45-day review period runs out before it can act — letting the funds lapse without an up-or-down vote. Government watchdogs consider it illegal; that hasn't stopped it from being used twice in two years.

Enterprise AI platform

A packaged set of a company's AI tools — assistants, coding agents, business-process automation — sold as a unified product to other businesses rather than to consumers. Meta bundling Muse and its developer tools this way, under a newly hired chief, signals it now sees enterprise customers as a distinct business to be won, not a side effect of consumer AI.

Habermas's Theory of Truth Meant He Changed His Mind

Jürgen Habermas built his career on a 'consensus' theory holding that a statement is true only if it would survive an ideal, unconstrained conversation among everyone affected — then, confronted with its problems, revised the theory rather than defending it out of loyalty. Carlo Rovelli Takes Aim at Analytic Philosophy's Naive Realism

Physicist Carlo Rovelli argues that a strain of recent analytic philosophy, in reacting against overreach in philosophy of mind, has swung back toward a naive realism that ignores what Einstein and Bohr's physics actually implies about the nature of reality. His target is method, not mysticism — a complaint that philosophy keeps re-litigating intuitions physics has already overturned.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Consensus theory of truth

The view, associated with Jürgen Habermas, that a statement counts as true if and only if it would be agreed to by everyone in a free, unconstrained, sufficiently informed discussion — making truth a matter of ideal agreement rather than correspondence to some mind-independent fact.

Naive realism

The view that the world is more or less exactly as it appears to ordinary perception and common sense — objects have the properties they seem to have, independent of any observer. Physicists studying relativity and quantum mechanics have long argued this picture doesn't survive contact with the actual physics.
