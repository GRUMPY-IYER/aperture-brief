Anthropic says state-linked operators in Mali, China and Iran used Claude for surveillance

Anthropic's latest threat intelligence report says state-linked operators in Mali, China and Iran used Claude to build surveillance systems, including a Malian security consultant who used it to build a tool profiling citizens from mobile-carrier data. Why it matters for you: When your own abuse report becomes the primary evidence of how governments are misusing your product, transparency and detection tooling stop being optional PR — they're the product's actual safety net.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Threat intelligence report

A periodic disclosure, pioneered by cybersecurity firms, in which a company publishes how its own product was abused and how it responded. Anthropic now publishes one for Claude; the September edition is the first to detail state-linked surveillance use.

Frontier model pacing

The idea that labs building the most capable AI models could voluntarily slow how fast they release new capability, rather than racing each other, to leave more time for safety work. Altman's comments are the highest-profile floating of the idea by a lab actually setting the pace.

A fully recyclable tape, made from a biogenic polymer, outsticks duct tape

Virginia Tech chemists built a fully recyclable adhesive tape from a biogenic-lipoic-acid polymer that outperformed Scotch tape, masking tape and duct tape in stickiness tests. Tuning the ratio of its two monomers into what the team calls a 'Goldilocks region' let them combine strength with a monomaterial design that can be recycled rather than landfilled.

Phys.org

Webb telescope catches the rings of the asteroid Chariklo changing shape

A decade after astronomers found two faint rings around the small icy body Chariklo, new James Webb observations show them changing within a few years — the inner ring 50% denser, the outer one 60% fainter. Scientists don't yet know whether that is a lasting evolution or an artifact of observing at a new wavelength.

ScienceDaily

A pigment-free white material, inspired by snow and lotus leaves, repels water too

Researchers built a foam-based material that is intensely white and water-repellent without any titanium dioxide pigment or PFAS chemicals, copying how snow, clouds and lotus leaves scatter light through tiny pores and air gaps. The technique, called Deep Foam Photolithography, points to a pigment-free way to make bright coatings without the environmental costs of conventional whiteners.

Philosophy

Philosophy Now

Why classical liberals are wrong to treat Foucault as an enemy

Philosophy Now argues that classical liberals who treat Michel Foucault as an enemy are reading him secondhand: his real target wasn't political power in Madison's sense but institutions that claim to define truth itself. Read that way, Foucault's warnings about 'regimes of truth' look less like a wokeness blueprint than a liberal case for distrusting anyone who claims to speak for truth.

Philosophy Now

Hustle culture and the illusion of freedom

Drawing on Theodor Adorno, Philosophy Now argues that hustle culture's promise of empowerment is a modern 'illusion of freedom': people voluntarily subordinate themselves to the market and call it self-actualization. Freedom becomes indistinguishable from functioning well as a worker, the essay argues, which quietly translates system-level inequality into personal moral failure.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Regimes of truth

Michel Foucault's term for the institutional structures that determine what counts as true in a given era — not truth itself, but the power that decides what gets to pass as truth. Distrusting them, one Philosophy Now essay argues, is a liberal instinct as much as a radical one.

The illusion of freedom

Theodor Adorno's diagnosis of a freedom that only lets you choose how well you perform inside a system you never chose — hustle culture's 'be your own boss' ethos, in one reading, is this illusion wearing a new outfit.
