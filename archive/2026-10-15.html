FBI Arrests OpenAI Employee Accused of Hiding a Recording Device in Its Offices

Prosecutors say Saudi national Abdulrahman Alfozan hid a recording device in a USB port behind a television at OpenAI's San Francisco office before being fired and arrested; he faces up to five years if convicted of intercepting oral communications. TechCrunch

An Australian Startup Sends AI Agents Undercover to Get Scammed on Purpose

Apate.ai's AI agents pose as naive scam targets to draw out fraudsters' bank accounts, crypto wallets and phone numbers, then pass that intelligence to bank and telecom clients; the company says its agents have wasted 17 years of scammers' time across 2.5 million conversations. Commonwealth Bank of Australia, a customer, reported a 76% drop in publicly reported scam losses after deploying the tool.

Why it matters for you: Using AI agents as bait rather than just as a filter is a genuinely different fraud-defense architecture, worth knowing about if your product has to fight AI-powered scams at scale.

Science

Phys.org

A Superheavy Element's Bond Breaks the Periodic Table's Own Rule

Physicists measured the bond between seaborgium, element 106, and a carbon monoxide ligand, and found it weaker than the periodic table's trend predicts rather than stronger. Simple calculations that ignore relativity expected the bond to keep strengthening going down the group, but accounting for relativistic effects on seaborgium's electron shells explains the reversal, work the researchers say helps test how well quantum chemistry handles the heaviest elements.

ScienceDaily

Hubble Data May Show a Planet Born From the Wreckage of a Dead Star

Reanalysis of a 1999 Hubble observation of the white dwarf HS 0209+0832 attributes a long-unidentified chemical signature to niobium, and a new study proposes that material expelled as the star died could have coalesced into a second-generation gas giant. The finding, published in Nature Astronomy, is explicitly tentative, but would be a rare glimpse of a planet forming after, rather than before, its star's death.

Aeon

How 'Lived Experience' Went From Romantic Ideal to Political Weapon

The essay traces 'lived experience' from the Romantics, who invoked it against a culture they thought distorted authentic feeling, to today's identity politics, where speaking from a group identity is often used to claim authority and demand deference. It holds the idea is both a genuine source of knowledge others lack and a danger to the solidarity it's meant to build.

Aeon

If Memory Fails, Are You Still You?

Charles Leadbeater uses dementia to test the Locke-and-Parfit view that memory is what makes you the same person over time, calling it powerful but dangerously partial. He argues a continental alternative, where identity rests on relationships and embeddedness in a shared world, better explains why people with dementia remain recognizably themselves.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Psychological continuity

The Locke-and-Parfit view that what makes you the same person over time is an unbroken chain of memory and psychology — the theory Charles Leadbeater argues dementia exposes as too partial an account of identity.

Standpoint claim

An argument that a person's identity or group membership gives their testimony special authority on a subject — the move at the center of the 'lived experience' debate in identity politics.
