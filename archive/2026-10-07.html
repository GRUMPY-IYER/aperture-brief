Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Mixture of experts (MoE)

An architecture that splits a large model into specialized sub-networks and routes each input to only a few of them, so a trillion-parameter model like Mistral's new release can run far cheaper than its full size implies.

Ecosystem lock-in

The way owning one device in a household makes the next one easier to sell — a smart lock from the same company as your phone talks to it more smoothly than a rival's, which is exactly why platform owners keep expanding into new device categories.

Philosophy

IAI News

Distrust the Ideas You Are Most Invested In

Philosopher Stephen Law argues that the more identity or effort we've sunk into a belief, the less our confidence in it tracks the actual evidence — and that this is exactly backwards from how we tend to treat our most cherished convictions. The fix isn't more certainty but a standing suspicion of the ideas we'd most hate to be wrong about.

Big Think

The Case That Madness Is Philosophy in Practice

Wouter Kusters, who writes from his own experience of psychosis, argues that reducing madness to brain chemistry captures its physical substrate but misses how the "mad" world is actually experienced from within — as a kind of accidental, lived philosophy rather than mere malfunction.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Motivated reasoning

The tendency to evaluate evidence more charitably when it supports a conclusion we're already invested in, and more skeptically when it doesn't — a bias that gets stronger, not weaker, the more identity we've attached to the belief.

Hegemony

A dominant power's claim to rightful leadership rather than mere strength — the framing a rising power often uses to describe its own ambitions, even as rivals read the same behavior as naked power-seeking.
