TechCrunch

Meta launches Muse, an AI agent it will let spend your money and send your messages

Muse can book travel, negotiate bills, shop online and handle scheduling for US adults, running on a model called Muse Spark inside an isolated 'Secure VM' with a separate Sentinel agent that must approve anything sent to the outside world. Meta's pitch is that explicit approval steps before spending money or messaging on a user's behalf make an autonomous agent trustworthy enough for daily errands.

Why it matters for you: The first mainstream agent with real purchasing and messaging authority makes Meta's permission-gate design the reference case to study before you ship anything with similar reach.

404 Media Investigative

A secret Border Patrol unit is mining Americans' financial data to trigger traffic stops

404 Media identified Predictive Intelligence Targeting Teams (PITT), secretive Border Patrol units in Washington and Texas that review financial and other law-enforcement-sensitive databases, then pass tips to local police who pull people over for unrelated pretexts. In one case, a PITT's read on a man's financial activity led to a Montana traffic stop and a DUI charge; DHS declined to say whether any of it required a warrant.

Why it matters for you: Predictive-policing logic applied to financial or behavioral data, with no disclosed warrant requirement, is a preview of what regulators will come looking for in any product that scores people on transaction or location signals.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Agentic AI

An AI system built to complete multi-step, real-world tasks on a user's behalf — booking, buying, messaging — rather than just answering questions. Meta's Muse is the most consumer-facing bet yet that people will hand agents real purchasing and communication authority.

Predictive policing

Using data analysis, rather than a specific suspicion or tip, to flag people or vehicles worth stopping or investigating. Border Patrol's PITT units apply the idea to financial records, feeding conclusions to local police who then find an unrelated reason to make the stop.

A crown-ether-studded framework pulls pure gold out of e-waste and mine wastewater

Chemists built a zirconium metal-organic framework threaded with crown ethers, ring-shaped molecules whose charged interiors selectively trap gold ions while ignoring interfering metals. Tested on dissolved computer chips and gold-mine wastewater, it recovered gold at up to 99.6% purity, including traces from seawater spiked at one part per billion.

Physicists directly observe gravity acting on a falling quantum object for the first time

An international team including Nobel laureate Roger Penrose split a quantum wave in two, let one half fall freely, then recombined them to measure exactly how gravity had shifted the falling half. The result confirms a long-predicted effect and shows Einstein's account of gravity still holds up under quantum conditions.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Analytic philosophy

The dominant Anglo-American philosophical tradition, originally defined by close attention to language and logical analysis. This week's Philosophy Now essay argues the label has outlived the method that once defined it, as its practitioners now range over nearly every philosophical question.

Existentialism

The postwar philosophical movement, associated with Sartre and de Beauvoir, holding that existence precedes essence — that people create their own meaning through choices rather than discovering a pre-given purpose. The two built the movement's central ideas largely in parallel rather than as a single joint project.
