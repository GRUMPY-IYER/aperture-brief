Hackers Who Stole FBI Employee Data Say They Won't Publish It

TechCrunch

A Popular Open-Source AI Gateway Was Compromised With an Infostealer

A malicious version of LiteLLM, an open-source proxy many companies use to route requests across AI model providers, was published to PyPI carrying an infostealer and remote-access trojan before being quarantined. The payload was hidden only in the packaged file, so reviewing the public source code would not have caught it.

Why it matters for you: LiteLLM routes requests across model providers for thousands of AI products; a poisoned release there is a reminder that your AI stack's attack surface includes every gateway library you didn't write.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Supply-chain attack

An attack that poisons a shared tool or library rather than its users directly, so one tampered package — like this month's fake LiteLLM release — can quietly reach every company that depends on it.

PII

Shorthand for personally identifiable information — names, addresses, phone numbers — which is exactly what made the FBI data breach dangerous: not classified secrets, but enough to locate and pressure real people.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Lottocracy

The idea of governing through citizen panels chosen at random, like a jury, instead of elections — proposed as a more genuinely democratic alternative to rule by elected or technocratic elites.

Hard problem of consciousness

The puzzle of why physical brain processes come with subjective experience at all, not just how they produce behavior — the deeper question some philosophers argue neuroscience keeps sidestepping rather than solving.
