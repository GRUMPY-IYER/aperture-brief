Trump says the only AI guardrail the US needs is him

Trump rejected calls for AI regulation and criticized Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's weekend essay urging the industry to slow down, saying 'a strong and smart president' is the only guardrail AI needs. OpenAI's Sam Altman and Elon Musk had both voiced support for Amodei's call to pace development.

404 Media Investigative

Cops searched Flock's surveillance cameras and typed 'LMAO' as the reason

An EFF review of Flock license-plate-reader searches found police typing 'LMAO,' 'idk,' 'asdfg' and slurs into the system's mandatory reason field while querying networks spanning thousands of cameras and hundreds of cities. Flock changed how the reason box works in late 2025, but the searches reviewed span 2023 through then.

Why it matters for you: A mandatory 'reason' field is only as good as the audit process behind it — a lesson for anyone building compliance logging into a surveillance or data-access product.

Waymo opened driverless rides to the public in Las Vegas, its 15th US market, with a waitlist already past 100,000 sign-ups. The initial zone covers the Strip and major resorts, running dozens of its new Ojai minivans under a Nevada permit for up to 1,000 vehicles.

Why it matters for you: A 100,000-name waitlist for a market Waymo hasn't marketed shows demand has outrun the fleet — a supply problem, not a demand one.

French chemists developed a nickel-catalyzed method to precisely swap hydrogen for deuterium in existing drug molecules, rather than resynthesizing them from scratch. Big Think

Philosophy has a trick for decisions where every option is defensible

Philosopher Ruth Chang's work on 'hard choices' argues that the hardest decisions aren't between a clearly better and worse option, but between options that are genuinely on a par. Chang suggests treating the choice as an occasion to decide who you want to be, rather than searching for a hidden right answer.

IAI News

Two people can look at the same evidence and reasonably live in different realities

Sociologist Steve Fuller argues that developments across neuroscience, quantum physics and AI undermine the assumption of one shared, observer-independent reality that people merely interpret differently. If he's right, some of today's fiercest disagreements aren't about values alone — they're about which reality each side takes as given.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Hard choices

Philosopher Ruth Chang's term for decisions between options that are genuinely comparable in value but not equal — neither better nor worse, just different — so no amount of information resolves them.

Perspectivism

The view that there's no single 'view from nowhere' — that what counts as real or true is inseparable from a particular standpoint. Nietzsche's version was about values; Fuller's is aimed at physics and AI.
