Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Age-prediction AI

Software that guesses how old you probably are by looking at patterns — what you search for, when you're online, how you write — rather than asking for ID. Apps increasingly use it to automatically route suspected minors into more restricted, "safer" versions of a product.

AI guardrails

The rules and filters built around an AI system to stop it from doing certain things — like discussing self-harm with a teenager, or generating a nonconsensual image. Guardrails are never perfect; determined users routinely probe for the gaps, which is why companies keep adjusting them.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Substrate independence

The idea that consciousness might not care what it's "made of" — that a mind built from silicon, or something even stranger, could in principle be just as conscious as one made of neurons, so long as it has the right kind of organized complexity. It's the philosophical backbone behind asking whether an alien, or an AI, could really have experiences.

Epistemology

The branch of philosophy concerned with what knowledge is and how we get it — not just what's true, but how we can justify believing it's true. It's the natural lens for questions like: if an AI hands you an answer with no visible reasoning or sources, do you actually know anything, or have you just been told something?
