Anthropic Prices Its IPO at Up to $1.4 Trillion — Short of Its Old $2 Trillion Target

Anthropic set a $70-$78 price range for its Nasdaq debut under the ticker 'ANT,' implying up to $1.404 trillion fully diluted — below the roughly $2 trillion it once targeted, but above its $965 billion valuation in May. Regulators Clear the First Small Nuclear Reactors at a US Industrial Site — but Not Until the 2030s

The NRC approved a construction permit for four of X-energy's Xe-100 small modular reactors at a Dow chemical plant in Texas, the first such approval at a US industrial site, though the reactors won't run until the early 2030s. Why it matters for you: A reactor approved today doesn't generate a watt until the early 2030s — any roadmap betting that 'AI power constraints will ease soon' needs to price in that multi-year lag, not read the headline as supply arriving.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Small modular reactor (SMR)

A nuclear reactor built far smaller than a traditional plant — 80 megawatts here — so it can sit at a single factory or data center instead of needing a dedicated utility-scale facility. None operate in the US yet; the earliest are years away.

Dual-class stock

A share structure where public investors get economic ownership but a separate class of founder-held shares keeps voting control — the structure behind Anthropic's IPO, where Class F shares carry 50.1% of the vote, leaving ordinary Class A buyers almost no governance say.

ScienceDaily

Hubble Captures Visible-Light Proof of a Planetary Collision's Aftermath

University of Arizona astronomers used Hubble to photograph the 'Cat's Tail,' a 93-billion-mile dark dust structure around the young star Beta Pictoris, for the first time in visible light after JWST had only seen it in infrared. The team says it's likely debris from a collision between Pluto-sized bodies, and found Hubble also caught a separate warp in the star's dust halo, probably caused by a giant planet ten times Jupiter's mass.

Phys.org

Purple Bacteria Turn CO2 and Industrial Waste Into a Billion-Dollar Supplement Ingredient

University of Valladolid researchers found purple phototrophic bacteria can produce coenzyme Q10, a popular energy supplement, and carotenoid pigments from CO2 and industrial waste gases instead of sugar feedstock. Their best strain, Rhodospirillum rubrum, hit CoQ10 yields approaching those of genetically engineered strains, though the authors stress this is lab-scale proof of concept, not yet commercial production.

ScienceDaily

Fossil Fuel Emissions Are Set to Dip This Year, but Total CO2 Still Hits a Record

The Global Carbon Budget 2026, compiled by more than 140 scientists led by Exeter's Pierre Friedlingstein, projects fossil CO2 emissions will fall 0.4% this year, partly from an oil-demand shock tied to the Iran conflict, while deforestation pushes total emissions to a record 43.3 billion tons. Only about 150 billion tons remain in the 1.5°C budget — under four years at current rates — and researchers warn a geopolitically driven dip isn't a real peak.

Philosophy

Aeon

Why Astrophysics Needs to Stop Treating Ordinary Matter as a 'Nuisance'

Physicist Dylan Jow argues astrophysics unfairly treats real phenomena — dust, gas, ordinary matter — as nuisances obscuring the 'Big Questions' of dark energy and fundamental physics, a hierarchy he traces to Cold War-era physics funding rather than any inevitable truth. He argues the field should recover astronomy's older value as the study of real places and origins, not a cut-rate stand-in for a particle accelerator.

Aeon

In Praise of the Dilettante, the Anti-Hero of an Over-Optimised Age

Philosopher Jay Miller defends the dilettante — the multi-interest amateur dismissed since Goethe and Schiller as a mere dabbler — as a legitimate way of life rooted in curiosity, blending Kierkegaard's rotating aesthete with Nietzsche's restless free spirit. He concedes the path forfeits recognition and mastery, but argues a specialization-obsessed age needs defenders of breadth over acclaim.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Big Questions

Astrophysics' informal ranking of research priorities — dark energy and fundamental physics first, everything else treated as a 'nuisance' — which philosopher Dylan Jow argues is a product of 20th-century funding politics, not a fact about what's worth studying.

Dilettante

Originally Baroque Italian for someone who pursued an art purely for delight, the word curdled into an insult for shallow amateurism — philosopher Jay Miller argues it deserves rehabilitating as a deliberate alternative to specialization.
