Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Red-teaming (AI safety testing)

Before releasing a powerful new model like OpenAI's Astra, labs run it through deliberate stress tests — trying to get it to do dangerous things (write malware, help plan an attack) to see where it breaks. If the model turns out too capable in a risky area, like today's story, that testing can delay the whole release, the same way a car doesn't ship until it passes a crash test.

Spinout

When senior employees leave a big company to start their own venture around an idea they couldn't pursue internally, it's called a spinout — like Jeff Dean's team leaving Google for Discovery Loop. It's different from being laid off: spinouts usually happen because the talent believes the idea deserves to be its own company, not a side project.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
The Hard Problem of Consciousness

Scientists can explain a lot about the brain — which neurons fire when you see red, which circuits light up when you feel pain. That's the "easy" problem. The "hard problem," a term coined by philosopher David Chalmers, is explaining why any of that physical activity is accompanied by subjective experience at all — why there's something it's like to see red, rather than the brain just processing red as data with nobody home to feel it. Today's Goff and Trumbull pieces are both, in different ways, wrestling with this same gap.

Non-duality (Advaita)

Advaita Vedanta — the tradition behind today's Prabuddha Bharata editorial — holds that the sense of being a separate self, cut off from everything else, is a kind of mistaken perception. "Non-dual" means "not two": at the deepest level, the individual self (atman) and the ultimate reality (Brahman) are said to be one and the same, and spiritual practice is less about acquiring new beliefs than about seeing through the illusion of separateness.
