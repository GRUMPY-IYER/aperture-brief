Justice Department Subpoenas California Election Workers in Noncitizen Voter Dispute

The Justice Department subpoenaed employees of the Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder's office, ordering at least two to testify November 4, a day after a federal judge rejected DOJ's demand that the county set aside ballots from 20,000 voters it flagged as likely noncitizens. Alaska's Pivotal Senate Race Roiled by Report of a Democratic Plot to Recruit a Spoiler Candidate

A New York Times report alleges Democratic operatives, including aides to Mary Peltola's campaign, tried to recruit a second candidate named Dan Sullivan to split votes from the Republican incumbent in Alaska's toss-up Senate race. Peltola's campaign denies involvement; Gov. Mike Dunleavy ordered a state investigation, and DOJ amended its existing election lawsuit to cite the report.

How ICE Operates in the Shadows of the Civil Immigration System

Reporting with The Marshall Project traces Pedro Vasquez Perdomo's arrest by masked ICE agents at a Pasadena bus stop and his transfer to a downtown LA processing site so crowded lawyers called it a 'black hole.' Because immigration detention is civil, not criminal, detainees often get no lawyer and little notice before being moved — a gap a federal judge has only partly curbed since.

Binance Pauses Withdrawals as Hackers Suspected of Draining Billions From Its Hot Wallets

Binance confirmed a security incident and suspended withdrawals after users reported funds draining from its wallets, with crypto trackers estimating $2 billion to $5.7 billion taken. Tether froze about $1.56 billion and Circle froze $800 million tied to the hack; the attackers remain unidentified.

Why it matters for you: Tether and Circle could freeze over $2 billion of the stolen funds within hours — a reminder that stablecoin issuers, not exchanges, are often the real circuit breaker when crypto custody fails.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Hot wallet

A cryptocurrency wallet connected to the internet for quick trading, as opposed to a 'cold' wallet kept offline. Hot wallets are convenient but are the ones hackers can actually reach, which is why exchanges like Binance keep most customer funds elsewhere.

Phys.org

A New Catalytic Process Recycles Silicone Waste Into Reusable Building Blocks at Up to 99% Yield

Chemists at the Technical University of Berlin, led by Martin Oestreich, broke down silicone waste from everyday products into dimethyldichlorosilane, a building block for new silicone, using a silylium-ion catalyst. Yields reached 99% for single products and 86% for a mixed batch, offering a path around silicone's usual fate of incineration or landfill; the team has filed a patent.

NASA

NASA's Dragonfly Rotorcraft Completes Its First Simulated Flight Over Titan's Dunes

Engineers ran the most complete test yet of Dragonfly's flight sequence on a Johns Hopkins APL testbed, simulating a flight over Titan-like dunes, a safe-landing-site search and touchdown using real flight software. The craft, launching no earlier than 2028 and arriving at Titan in 2034, must fly itself with no real-time help from Earth.

Aeon

What Physics Can Teach You About Surviving a Messy Life

Zahaan Bharmal, writing after being fired from his first job, argues physics's real value isn't reductionist bragging rights but its models of chaotic systems — which teach that resilience needs surge capacity, and that people tolerate inequality only when a game seems fair. Used humbly, he argues, physics helps us question assumptions and sit with uncertainty.

Aeon

Why Real Peace Requires Rebuilding the Economy, Not Just Signing a Treaty

Azize Aslan argues postwar peace deals routinely fail because they stop direct violence without dismantling the economic structures that fueled the conflict. Drawing on worker cooperatives in Rojava and Colombia's ECOMÚN, she contends lasting peace needs democratically run economies that meet people's needs daily, not a document signed once.

Aeon

Living With Housemates Is Where Your Self-Illusions Go to Die

Elizabeth Oldfield traces today's loneliness epidemic partly to individualism's prizing of privacy and autonomy, arguing from her own household of housemates that being witnessed at our worst by people who stay anyway reshapes character in ways solitude can't. Such communities last, she suggests, only when anchored to a purpose beyond themselves.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Surge capacity

A system's reserve ability to absorb a sudden shock without breaking — a concept from physics and engineering that today's essay borrows to describe what makes a person, not just a bridge or a power grid, resilient under stress.

Cooperative economy

An economy organized around worker- or community-owned enterprises run democratically for collective benefit, rather than investor-owned firms run for profit — the model today's essay on postwar peace points to as more durable than a signed ceasefire.
