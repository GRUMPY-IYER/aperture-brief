Ballot Rejections Soared in Rural Counties After USPS Reduced Mail Service

A ProPublica analysis found rejection rates for late-arriving mail ballots have doubled or tripled in some rural counties compared with the 2022 primary, as Postal Service consolidation routes ballots through processing centers farther from the communities that mail them. Some affected counties are adding drop boxes, but the pattern threatens to disenfranchise rural voters weeks before the midterms.

404 Media Investigative

A City Found Flock Had Accessed a Kids' Gymnastics Room's Cameras as a Sales Demo. It Renewed the Contract Anyway.

A city discovered that Flock Safety had accessed its camera feed, including inside a children's gymnastics facility, to use as a sales demonstration for a prospective client, 404 Media reported. Despite the discovery, the city renewed its contract with the surveillance company rather than imposing new restrictions on vendor access to its footage.

Why it matters for you: Vendor access logs are the only reason this surfaced at all — if your own surveillance or data vendors can reach a customer's feed 'just to show a prospect what's possible,' that access exists whether or not anyone ever audits it.

TechCrunch

Grok Releases the First Seven Minutes of Its AI-Generated 'Odyssey' — and It Shows

xAI's Grok published roughly seven minutes of an AI-generated adaptation of 'The Odyssey,' three months after Elon Musk first announced a full-length film, now billed as "Episode 1" of an ongoing series. Why it matters for you: A flagship 'AI movie' landing as seven rough minutes three months after its announcement is a useful reality check on how far generative video still lags the demos built to sell it.

Third-party data access

The behind-the-scenes ability a vendor's own staff have to view or pull data from a product they sell you — distinct from what the product's privacy policy promises end users, and often invisible until something like a sales demo exposes it.

Science

ScienceDaily

A Century-Old Chemistry Rule Taught in Every Textbook May Be Wrong

A new study in the Journal of Chemical Education finds that the inductive effect — taught for nearly a century as weakening gradually across three or four bonds — actually stops after the first bond in neutral molecules, based on modern computational modeling. Two UK A-level exam boards have already announced reviews of how they teach the concept, citing the research directly.

NASA

Perseverance Breaks Opportunity's Mars Driving Record

NASA's Perseverance rover passed 28.10 miles (45.22 km) driven on Mars on October 11, edging past the 28.06-mile record set by Opportunity over nearly 15 years — a distance Perseverance covered in about 5.5 years. Its AutoNav self-driving system handled over 90% of that total, and NASA expects the rover to keep extending the record through its newly approved mission extension.

Philosophy

Aeon

The Century-Long Search for the Limits of Knowledge

Physicists Timothy Andersen and Alexander V Gheorghiu argue that truth in science and logic isn't a fixed ceiling but something that shifts as our knowledge and language evolve, tracing a century of attempts to pin down the limits of what can be known. The essay treats the search itself, not any final answer, as the real subject.

Big Think

3 Philosophers on What Humans Should Never Give Up to AI

Three philosophers tell Big Think what people should guard most carefully as AI takes on more thinking: the capacity to think for themselves, curiosity about open-ended questions, and a feel for what makes any one person's perspective irreplaceable. None frames the stakes as a question about machine consciousness, but about what human thinking is for in the first place.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Epistemology

The branch of philosophy concerned with what we can know and how we know it — the subject behind debates over whether truth is a fixed target or something that shifts as our concepts and evidence evolve.

Anthropocentrism

The assumption that human traits or interests are the natural standard against which everything else, including a machine's thinking, should be measured — a premise some philosophers of AI argue needs examining rather than assuming.
