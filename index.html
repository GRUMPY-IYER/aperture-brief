When the US Military Adopted AI Targeting, Civilian Deaths Doubled in Somalia

Airwars data analyzed by The Intercept shows strikes in Somalia nearly tripled and civilian casualties more than tripled in the two years after the Pentagon's Project Maven targeting AI arrived, with no evidence the system made strikes more accurate. Anthropic's IPO Prices at $83 a Share, the Largest Offering on Record

Anthropic priced its IPO above its $70-78 range, raising about $81.3 billion at a $1.5 trillion valuation and topping SpaceX as the largest offering ever — with Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and other chipmakers buying $26 billion of non-voting shares alongside the public float. TechCrunch

Nadella: Much of the World's Critical Infrastructure Is 'Unpatchable,' and AI Is Making That Dangerous

At the DealBook Summit, the Microsoft CEO said newly internet-connected systems like data-center chillers and HVAC firmware often can't be patched at all, even as AI tools find exploitable bugs faster than teams can safely test fixes. He described this week's Outlook outage as a "latent bug" meeting a "perfect storm," with patches now needing to reach production almost instantly because they're being exploited in the wild.

Why it matters for you: This is a hardware generation gap colliding with a software-speed problem: devices built with no patch path at all are now facing attackers that move at AI speed, which is a design failure no amount of faster incident response can fully compensate for.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Non-voting shares

Stock that shares in a company's profits but carries no say in how it's run. Chipmakers and cloud giants buying $26 billion of Anthropic's IPO this way got exposure to its upside without any claim on its governance — a sign of how much leverage a hot IPO can retain over even its biggest buyers.

External foundry customer

A company that pays a chipmaker to manufacture chips it designed itself, rather than buying chips designed in-house. Intel has spent years trying to build this business to compete with TSMC; landing an unnamed customer for its advanced 18A-P process is the first real evidence it can.

ScienceDaily

A Battery Catalyst Made From Vitamin B12 Just Survived 250 Charge Cycles

University of Córdoba chemists bonded vitamin B12 to carbon nanotubes to build the first rechargeable zinc-air battery catalyzed by the vitamin, anchoring it so it survives the water-based electrolyte that normally dissolves it. The cobalt at B12's core drives both the discharge and recharge reactions, a dual role needed for a rechargeable design and a step toward cheaper alternatives to lithium-ion for storing solar and wind power.

ScienceDaily

NASA Pushed Mars Helicopter Blades Past the Speed of Sound Without Them Breaking

JPL engineers spun experimental rotor blades to Mach 1.08 in a chamber simulating Mars's thin atmosphere, well past the limits that kept the Ingenuity helicopter's blades at a cautious 2,700 RPM across its 72 flights. The result, feeding into the SkyFall helicopter mission due in 2028, could let future Mars rotorcraft carry heavier instruments and fly farther, since thin air forces rotors to spin far faster than on Earth to generate the same lift.

Phys.org

Homo Erectus Was Still Living in Dense Chinese Rainforest 184,000 Years Ago, Fossil Teeth Show

Eight teeth from two Guangxi caves, dated by five independent methods, are the youngest confirmed Homo erectus fossils in mainland China and show the species thrived in tropical rainforest long thought survivable only by Homo sapiens. Chemical traces suggest some individuals ranged widely through forested terrain as children, complicating the assumption that rainforests were a dead end for every human lineage except our own.

Aeon

Habermas Built a Theory of Democracy on Staying Open to Persuasion. Did He Actually Practice It?

Emilie Prattico, once his student, traces how Jürgen Habermas revised his own theory over decades: conceding that the bourgeois "public sphere" he idealized had excluded women, and abandoning his earlier claim that truth is simply what people would agree to under fair conditions. In his later work he warned that social media splinters the public into self-enclosed groups, calling a shared, inclusive public sphere a "constitutional imperative" rather than an optional nice-to-have.

Aeon

A Finance Academic's Diagnosis for Ailing Democracies: It Wasn't Bad Luck, It Was Policy

Les Coleman argues that neoliberal reforms dating to Reagan and Thatcher systematically weakened democratic institutions, correlating with higher government debt, slower growth and doubled household debt across OECD countries while wealth concentration let elites shape politics directly. His fixes range from higher politician pay and democracy vouchers to a progressive death tax and an independent economic-crime police unit modeled on Singapore's.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Public sphere

Habermas's name for the shared space where citizens reason together and hold power accountable through open debate. He later conceded its classic 18th-century version quietly excluded women, and warned that social media is now splintering it into self-enclosed groups that no longer argue from shared facts.

Structure-conduct-performance

An economics framework linking how an industry or system is structured, to how its participants behave, to what outcomes result. Coleman uses it to argue that deregulation-era policy choices structurally produced today's debt and inequality, rather than those outcomes arriving by chance.
