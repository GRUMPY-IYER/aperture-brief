404 Media Investigative

Data Centers Are Landing in Black and Hispanic Neighborhoods at More Than Twice the Rate of White Ones, Study Finds

A study of roughly 81,000 census tracts and 5,291 data centers found majority-Black neighborhoods are 2.2 times as likely to host one as white neighborhoods, rising to 4.2 times for majority-Asian neighborhoods, even after controlling for income, education and land costs. Researchers trace much of the pattern to redlining-era transmission-line routing that data centers now cluster around, from pollution complaints near xAI's Memphis turbines to a Taylor, Texas, park slated for a data center next to a Black neighborhood.

Why it matters for you: For anyone siting infrastructure, this is a preview of coming scrutiny: voters have already rejected data centers at the ballot box in some races, so equity impact and community engagement are becoming delivery constraints on a project timeline, not just PR considerations.

TechCrunch

An Anthropic Mathematician Says He and Claude Solved the 167-Year-Old Riemann Hypothesis

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says he worked with Claude to produce a Lean-formalized proof of the Riemann hypothesis, one of mathematics' most famous unsolved problems, and other mathematicians confirmed the formal proof compiles — though no one has yet produced a human-readable explanation of why it works. The claim arrived the same week a Stanford postdoc separately posted an unformalized, human-only proof of a generalized version, as the mathematical community works through both.

Why it matters for you: Formal verification let the field check a startling claim within days instead of months of peer review — a pattern worth borrowing for any AI-generated output in a domain where correctness can be checked mechanically, even though 'it compiles' and 'we understand it' remain two different things.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Infrastructure redlining

The pattern where new infrastructure — like data centers — clusters in the same neighborhoods that historic, racially discriminatory redlining once shaped, often because it follows transmission lines or land that was cheap for the same underlying reasons. The 404 Media study found this pattern holds for data centers even after controlling for income and education.

Formal verification (Lean)

A way of checking a mathematical proof by writing it in a language, like Lean, that software can verify step by step for logical validity. It can confirm a proof is internally consistent far faster than human peer review, but it doesn't by itself tell anyone why the proof works.

ScienceDaily

Voyager 1 Is Now So Far Away a Signal Takes a Full Day to Reach It

NASA's Voyager 1, launched in 1977, has become the first spacecraft to sit a full light-day from Earth — about 16.1 billion miles away — so a radio signal now takes 24 hours each way. The nearly 50-year-old probe hasn't returned usable science data since November 14, and engineers are racing to restore its data flow while rationing a nuclear power supply expected to keep its remaining instruments running only until around summer 2027.

Phys.org

A Chip Holding a Million Microcrystals Reveals Why Some Semiconductors Form Layered Cores and Shells

Northwestern chemists built a chip patterned with a million microscopic wells to rapidly map how cesium-based, lead-free semiconductor mixtures crystallize, finding that some recipes separate into bromine-rich cores wrapped in iodine-rich shells. The layered core structure explains why these materials sometimes have unexpectedly small bandgaps despite higher bromine content, and the bromine-rich regions also resisted humid-air degradation far better — a clue for building cheaper, more stable solar cells and LEDs.

Phys.org

Bumblebees Solve Puzzles With Under a Million Neurons, Challenging Assumptions About Brain Size and Intelligence

Bumblebees, whose brains hold fewer than a million neurons, can move objects to reach nectar and learn to solve puzzles by watching other bees, and a review by two neuroscientists argues their oversized memory and navigation circuits — not bigger brains — explain it. The authors say sophisticated cognition may come from refined wiring rather than entirely new neural machinery, challenging the assumption that intelligence scales mainly with brain size.

Aeon

What If Civilisation Was Built on Horseback, Not on Grain?

Historian Lhamsuren Munkh-Erdene argues the first states arose not from grain surpluses but from 'extrahuman transportation' — horses, boats, chariots — that let mobile pastoralists project power and conquer settled farming societies, from Bronze Age Sintashta to the Comanche. He traces the rival 'grain thesis' to a Cold War-era paradigm built to support territorial claims over Inner Asia, and argues it has wrongly erased the steppe's central role in building the earliest empires.

Aeon

Astronomy Keeps Dismissing Its Strangest Discoveries as 'Nuisances' in the Hunt for Bigger Questions

Physicist Dylan Jow argues that astronomy has been reorganized around fundamental physics' 'Big Questions' — dark energy, cosmic dawn — so interstellar dust and gas get treated as 'nuisances' to be subtracted away rather than studied for their own sake. He traces this hierarchy to Cold War-era funding shifts and the loss of the Superconducting Super Collider, arguing it's a historical accident rather than a natural order, and that reclaiming the 'nuisance' might restore astronomy's oldest appeal: wondering where we come from.

Aeon

When an AI Companion Is the Only Thing That Remembers a Child's Day

Psychoanalyst Tom Wooldridge argues the real question about children and AI companions isn't whether they displace human relationships, but what they offer the growing number of children with inadequate emotional support — and what they cannot. Drawing on attachment theory, he argues AI can approximate the consistency a child needs, but can never provide the mutuality of being refused and discovering another mind that exists independently of her wishes, leaving open what we owe the children it currently holds.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Conquest theory

The idea that early states arose when mobile, horse- or boat-enabled pastoralist groups conquered and ruled over settled farming populations, rather than farming itself producing the surplus that created states. It challenges the more familiar 'grain thesis,' which credits agricultural surplus with giving rise to the first governments.

Mutuality

Psychoanalyst Jessica Benjamin's idea that a real relationship requires two independent centers of experience, each affected by but not controlled by the other. Wooldridge argues an AI companion, however responsive, can't yet provide this — it is never changed by the child the way another person is.
