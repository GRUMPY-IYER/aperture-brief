France Blames Iran for Hacks That Spoiled Food at Supermarkets and Reached Blood Banks

Hackers French officials tie to Iran's Revolutionary Guard broke into internet-connected refrigeration controllers in two attack waves, spoiling food at supermarkets nationwide and reaching blood banks before the French Blood Establishment stopped them. Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Recall query

A federal step short of ordering a new recall — regulators formally ask an automaker to prove that a safety fix it already issued actually worked, as NHTSA is now doing with Waymo's school-bus software update.

Industrial control system (ICS)

Internet-connected hardware controllers that run physical equipment like refrigeration units and pumps — often old, rarely patched, and increasingly targeted by state-linked hackers because breaking in changes the real world, not just a database.

ScienceDaily

A Clogged Filter Could Cut Oil Refining's Energy Use by a Third

KAIST and Georgia Tech researchers found that letting crude oil foul a cheap polymer membrane actually improves it: heavier hydrocarbons narrow the pores to under 2 nanometers, letting lighter fractions like gasoline pass through 23 times faster than earlier membranes, at room temperature instead of 350°C. Simulations project roughly 32% less energy use and 38% less CO2 if refineries adopt it.

ScienceDaily

Physicists Coax Light Into Acting Like a Solid and a Liquid at Once

An international team used a liquid-crystal microcavity to create a 'supersolid' from light-matter particles called exciton polaritons, a state with both a repeating crystal structure and frictionless superfluid flow. Unlike the 2017 original, made near absolute zero, this version works at nearly 60°C and can be tuned electrically, opening it to optical and neuromorphic computing research.

Aeon

Why 'Equality' Means So Many Different Things That It Now Divides Us

Oxford political theorist Teresa Bejan traces how 'equality' has carried contradictory meanings since antiquity — evenness rather than sameness, equal before the law but not in social rank — and argues that ambiguity, not clarity, has always given the word its political force. She argues equality becomes real not through better definitions but through practices, like jury service, where people act as peers despite their differences.

Aeon

If Intelligence Is Just Optimisation, What Happens to Human Dignity?

Philosopher Sasha Mudd challenges the AI-research habit of defining intelligence as optimisation, which she traces to Hume's view of reason as a servant of desire. Following Kant, she argues real intelligence can question which goals are worth pursuing at all — and that losing sight of this risks people starting to treat themselves as optimisable tools, not just their machines.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Equality of what?

Philosophers mostly agree people start out equal, but endlessly disagree on equality of what — opportunity, outcome, treatment, standing. That unresolved question is why 'equality' can be invoked by opposite sides of the same argument.

Instrumental reason

Hume's idea that reason only serves whatever goals your desires hand it, never questioning the goals themselves — the model many AI systems run on, and the one Kant's alternative (reason that can judge ends, not just pursue them) pushes back against.
