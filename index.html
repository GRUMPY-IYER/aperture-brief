Nvidia's Board Formally Rules Out Bringing Jensen Huang Back, as the SEC Opens a Probe

A Five-Day Wave of Outages at Microsoft, the FAA, JPMorgan and Apple Has One Common Thread: Patches Rushed Out Under AI-Discovered Vulnerabilities

Why it matters for you: This is patch velocity outrunning change management: when a discovery tool gets faster than the release process built around it, the bottleneck shifts from finding the bug to shipping the fix without breaking something else — the same tradeoff any PM faces scaling a team's shipping cadence past its testing capacity.

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Patch velocity vs. change management

How fast a team can ship a fix for a newly found bug, versus how carefully it tests that fix before shipping. AI tools are now finding vulnerabilities faster than organizations can safely verify patches for them — this week's outage wave at Microsoft, the FAA, JPMorgan and Apple is what happens when the first number outpaces the second.

Export-control compliance committee

A standing board subcommittee set up to vet a company's deals against export-control law before they happen, instead of explaining them after a scandal. Nvidia created one the same day it exited China chip sales — a sign the board, not just management, now owns that risk.

ScienceDaily

A Mysterious Twist in the Universe's Oldest Light Could Reveal New Physics

UC San Diego physicists built a new method using Planck satellite data to test whether a hinted rotation in the cosmic microwave background's polarization — "cosmic birefringence" — is real or a detector-calibration artifact, finding a twist of 0.37°±0.12° that matches prior measurements. The result doesn't yet confirm new physics like axion-like fields or dark energy, but it rules out one major source of error that has dogged the measurement.

ScienceDaily

A New Material Could Solve Carbon Capture's Humidity Problem

Oregon State chemists built a metal-organic framework, nicknamed BVR-X, that physically keeps water and CO2 separated within its pores, so it doesn't lose capture ability in the humid exhaust most industrial smokestacks actually produce. Tested on a wet, 4%-CO2 stream over dozens of cycles, the material kept capturing CO2 without degrading — unlike most MOFs, which choke on moisture long before they see real flue gas.

Phys.org

Eagles Preying on Human-Sized Baboons Upend a Theory About Why Our Ancestors Stopped Fearing the Sky

Researchers found skeletal evidence — talon punctures, shoulder scrapes — that Verreaux's eagles kill adult male baboons weighing over 26 kg in Chad's Ennedi plateau, challenging the long-held assumption that early hominins grew too heavy for eagle predation once past about 13 kg. The finding revives the question of why group vigilance and cooperation evolved, if large-bodied primates were never actually safe from raptors overhead.

Philosophy

Aeon

Why an Empty Universe Isn't as Simple as Philosophers Assumed

Andrew T Brenner argues that truths of logic, mathematics and morality would still hold even in a world with nothing in it, so a universe containing one sufficiently simple thing — a God, or an initial cosmic singularity — that explains those truths can be simpler overall than a void that leaves them dangling unexplained. That doesn't settle why reality takes this particular form, but it undercuts the classic puzzle of why there's something rather than nothing.

Aeon

A 1646 Refusal to Kneel Shows Why 'Equality' Has Never Meant Just One Thing

Historian Teresa Bejan traces modern equality talk to 1646, when the agitator John Lilburne refused to kneel or remove his hat before the House of Lords, arguing God made every person "equal and alike in power, dignity, authority and majesty" — shifting equality from equal treatment under law to equal standing among peers. She argues the idea's very ambiguity is why it survived centuries of hierarchy, functioning less as a belief than a practice sustained by institutions like jury service.

Aeon

The Real Lamarck Wasn't About Giraffe Necks — and Biology Is Quietly Proving Him Right

Historian Jessica Riskin argues Lamarck is remembered only for the mocked giraffe-neck story, when his actual theory held that organisms actively transform themselves and their surroundings, driven by an inherent "power of life" pushing toward complexity — agency that neo-Darwinism later stripped out in favor of passive organisms shaped by random variation. She says recent work on epigenetics and niche construction is reviving that view, with organisms now shown to genuinely influence their own natural selection.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Necessary truth

A fact that would hold in absolutely any possible world, not just this one — like basic truths of logic or math. Brenner's essay turns on whether such truths can help explain why anything exists, or merely describe a universe that already does.

Niche construction

The idea that organisms don't just adapt to a fixed environment but actively reshape it — a beaver damming a river, a worm aerating soil — in ways that then reshape what future generations get selected for. It's one strand of research Riskin says is reviving Lamarck's long-dismissed view that life itself has agency.
