TechCrunch

Anthropic Warns It May Be 'Imminently' Close to AI That Can Build More Capable AI

In fine print of a revised S-1 filing, Anthropic said internal estimates suggest it may soon meet its own threshold for 'dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI' — recursive self-improvement, where AI gets good enough at training itself to compound its own capabilities. The company says it has not designated any current model as crossing that line and does not expect to let Claude build its successors without human oversight.

Why it matters for you: A frontier lab flagging this in a financial filing, not a safety paper, means governance and disclosure norms for AI capability jumps are about to become a live regulatory question.

Germany Says Lawmakers' iPhones May Have Been Hit by Spyware

Germany's federal cybersecurity office said members of the Bundestag and other prominent figures received Apple warnings of possible spyware targeting, in a campaign Apple says reached users in 99 countries. Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Recursive self-improvement (RSI)

The point at which an AI system becomes capable enough to meaningfully improve its own training process, letting its capabilities compound faster than human researchers alone could manage — the scenario AI safety researchers treat as a critical threshold to control, not race past.

Zero-click spyware

Surveillance software that can infect a phone without the owner clicking a link or opening a file, often sold by private 'mercenary spyware' vendors to governments. It's why Apple's threat notifications matter even though they can't say who sent the attack.

A Barium Catalyst Could Make Ammonia Without High Heat, Pressure or Precious Metals

Tokyo researchers built a cobalt-and-barium catalyst that weakens nitrogen's famously tough triple bond enough to synthesize ammonia at 300°C and modest pressure, outperforming benchmark ruthenium and industrial iron catalysts in their tests. IceCube's 'Ghost Particles' Carry 10 Billion Times the Energy of an Ordinary Neutrino

The IceCube Neutrino Observatory, a cubic kilometer of instrumented Antarctic ice, catches only a few dozen of the most energetic cosmic neutrinos a year — chargeless, near-massless particles light-years of lead couldn't reliably stop. The sources of the highest-energy ones, carrying roughly ten billion times the energy of a typical solar neutrino, remain unknown.

Jumping Spiders Twitch, Curl Their Legs and Move Their Eyes in a REM-Like Sleep State

University of Bonn researchers found that jumping spiders cycle between a still 'quiescence' stage and a twitchier, eye-moving stage resembling REM sleep, responding far slower to a loud noise during the latter. Spiders and vertebrates split evolutionarily some 500 million years ago, so structured sleep in both suggests the basic architecture may be very old.

Why Peacebuilding That Leaves Capitalism Intact Doesn't Actually Build Peace

Azize Aslan argues that standard peace deals stop at ending violence and leave intact the economic relations that caused the conflict, leaving ex-combatants asking 'what now?' after the guns fall silent. She points to Rojava's bottom-up cooperatives and Colombia's ECOMÚN federation as imperfect but real attempts to build peace as a daily, collective way of living rather than a signed document.

Aeon

Lamarck Said Living Things Remake Themselves and the World — Science Buried Him, Then Proved Him Partly Right

Jean-Baptiste Lamarck, who coined the word 'biology' in 1802, argued that organisms actively shape their own bodies and environments rather than passively obeying fixed laws — a view his rival Cuvier helped bury for over a century. Jessica Riskin traces how epigenetics, niche construction and inherited behavioral adaptation are now reviving a version of his 'active life' idea that 20th-century genetics had written off.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Negative vs. positive peace

A distinction from peace researcher Johan Galtung: negative peace just means no active violence, while positive peace means dismantling the unjust structures — economic, political, social — that caused the conflict in the first place. Most peace deals settle for the first and call it done.

Niche construction

The idea that organisms don't just adapt to their environment — they actively reshape it, and that reshaped environment then changes what traits get selected for next. A coral reef or a beaver dam is as much a product of evolution as the animal that built it.
