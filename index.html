Trump's Hollowing Out of the VA Is Already Killing Veterans in New Jersey

ProPublica finds thousands of doctors, nurses and other staff have left the VA since Trump's return, driven by canceled union contracts, return-to-office orders and buyouts. At New Jersey's East Orange VA, the losses have suspended radiation treatment and repeatedly closed operating rooms, and reporters tie the shortages to a Vietnam veteran's delayed appendix surgery and a cancer patient's delayed radiation, both fatal. The VA disputes any harm and points to record appointment numbers, but declined to make its leaders available for interviews.

A Lost Final Draft of 'I Have a Dream' Was Sitting in a Desk Drawer for Decades

Civil rights advocate Xernona Clayton found seven handwritten pages marked "speech" in a drawer belonging to her late husband Edward, a King aide, in the late 1990s — and didn't grasp for decades what they were. Now 96, she had them authenticated by Christie's as the only known handwritten final draft of "I Have a Dream," showing King revising in blue ink on August 27, 1963, wrestling with the phrase "tranquilizing drug of gradualism" before settling on "free at last." The pages will tour Atlanta and Chicago before a January auction expected to top $10 million, with proceeds split between Clayton's foundation and the King Center.

Nvidia's Board Weighs Ousting Jensen Huang Over a Second Affair, as His 33-Year Run Hangs in the Balance

TechCrunch

OpenAI's Quarterly Revenue Reportedly Tops $10 Billion for the First Time — Still Losing Money, Just Less of It

Bloomberg reports OpenAI's Q3 revenue crossed $10 billion for the first time, up nearly 50% on Q2's $6.7 billion, pushing its annualized run rate to $50 billion as its adjusted operating margin improved from -150% to -109%. Rival Anthropic reported $17 billion in Q3 revenue and an $81 billion run rate as of late October, though the two firms calculate the metric differently.

Why it matters for you: Operating margin improving from -150% to -109% is real progress and still deeply negative — watch the trend line, not the headline number, before concluding any AI lab's unit economics have turned a corner.

CoreWeave Is Borrowing Against Its Own Customer Contracts to Avoid a Bond Market Charging It Over 15%

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Revenue run rate

A company's most recent quarter's revenue multiplied out to a yearly pace — not actual annual revenue. It's how OpenAI's $10B quarter becomes a '$50B run rate,' and why run-rate comparisons between companies that calculate it differently, like OpenAI and Anthropic, can mislead.

Contract-backed financing

Borrowing against the cash a specific signed customer contract guarantees, rather than against the company as a whole — the structure CoreWeave is leaning on to fund its $132.5B order backlog after unsecured bond yields topped 15%.

ScienceDaily

A New Supercomputer Simulation Models the Universe's First 2 Billion Years in Unprecedented Detail

MIT and Harvard researchers built Lumina, a simulation spanning 1.6 billion light-years and the universe's first 2 billion years, the first to jointly model hydrogen and helium reionization. Run on Oak Ridge's Frontier supercomputer at roughly 30 times the resolution of its predecessor IllustrisTNG, it tracks half a trillion elements and will let astronomers test its predictions against real telescope data.

Phys.org

A 'Self-Healing' Catalyst Could Make Platinum-Free Hydrogen Production Practical

Tohoku University researchers found that cobalt phosphide, a cheap platinum alternative for splitting water into hydrogen, actually protects itself in use: hydroxyl and hydrogen-containing groups form on its surface and stop the metal dissolving away. Drawing on nearly 2,300 observations across 886 catalysts in the literature, the team ran it stable for 24 hours at high current density in acidic, neutral and alkaline conditions alike.

ScienceDaily

A Simple Model of Cells Chasing a Chemical Signal May Explain How Hair Knows Where to Grow

University of Geneva researchers built a mathematical model of chemotaxis — cells moving toward a chemical signal — that reproduced real hair-follicle patterns in both lab mice and spiny mice without a detailed developmental blueprint. The result challenges the long-dominant 'expansion-induction' theory of follicle spacing, and tuning the same model's parameters also reproduced the spiny mouse's unusually regular, aligned pattern.

Philosophy

Aeon

Why Keeping Church and State Separate Rests on a Difference Psychologists Call 'Credence' vs Belief

Philosophers Neil Van Leeuwen and Jacob Eisler argue religious conviction works psychologically unlike ordinary factual belief: it's chosen, resistant to evidence, and stays compartmentalized from daily action, while factual belief is involuntary and guides behavior everywhere. That difference, they argue, is exactly what makes the liberal bargain work — government shouldn't establish a religion because religious credence can't be settled by democratic evidence, and free exercise is safe because people can set it aside in public life.

Aeon

Lamarck Said Living Things Shape Their Own Evolution. A New Generation of Biologists Agrees.

Historian Jessica Riskin revisits Jean-Baptiste Lamarck's rejected idea that organisms actively transform themselves and their environments rather than merely responding to outside pressures, a view she says later neo-Darwinists purged partly in service of ideas like eugenics. Current research on epigenetic inheritance and niche construction is reviving it, with biologist Martha Muñoz arguing flatly that "organisms can influence evolution directly."

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Religious credence

A psychological category distinct from ordinary belief — chosen rather than forced by evidence, and kept separate from everyday action — that philosophers use to explain why democracies can let people hold religious convictions without those convictions ruling over law.

Niche construction

The idea, central to the Lamarckian revival, that organisms don't just adapt to their environment but actively reshape it — and that the reshaped environment then shapes their own evolution in turn.
