A Lost Georgia O'Keeffe Painting Spent 40 Years Hidden Behind Fur Coats in a Closet

In 1963, a young Foreign Service officer bought O'Keeffe's 'Near Abiquiu, New Mexico I' as a graduation gift from her parents — then hid it in her entry closet in the 1980s after noticing a bridge-game guest eyeing it too closely. She never mentioned it again until 2022, when she told her niece Betsy while preparing the apartment for home health aides; Betsy didn't actually see the painting until a few weeks ago, helping her aunt, who has since died, get ready for hospice. A Single Genetic Switch May Explain Why Primate Brains Got So Big

Taiwanese and Japanese researchers found that a DNA switch shared only by primates controls the gene DACH1, which governs how many brain-building 'basal radial glia' cells form during cortical development. Boosting that switch's activity in mouse and human brain organoids nearly quadrupled the share of those cells, suggesting evolution reshaped primate brains by rewiring old genes' instructions rather than inventing new ones.

A Brain-Scan Score Flags Alzheimer's Risk Years Before Any Symptoms Appear

Aeon

Gödel Didn't Prove There's a Ceiling on What We Can Know

UCL logician Alexander V Gheorghiu revisits a century-long argument — from Hilbert's confidence that every mathematical problem is solvable, through Gödel's incompleteness theorems, to later work on meaning and proof — and concludes truth isn't a fixed limit we keep failing to reach. Because our own linguistic and mathematical practices can be faithfully extended, he argues, truth grows as we build it rather than capping what we can know.

Aeon

Why Treating Intelligence as Optimization Misses What Reason Is For

Philosopher Sasha Mudd argues that defining intelligence as efficient optimization — the lens behind most AI — reduces reason to a tool for chasing goals we already have. Kant's alternative treats reason as the power to judge whether those goals are worth having at all, and Mudd warns that efficiency-obsessed AI risks teaching us to stop asking that question ourselves.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Incompleteness theorem

Gödel's famous result that any sufficiently powerful mathematical system contains true statements it cannot prove from within itself. It's often read as a hard ceiling on knowledge, but this essay argues it's really a sign that our systems of proof can always be extended further.

Instrumental reason

Treating reason as nothing more than a calculator for achieving whatever goal you already have — the implicit assumption behind judging AI by how efficiently it 'optimizes.' Kant's alternative says reason's real job is deciding which goals are worth having in the first place.
