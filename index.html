METR

Independent Auditor Finds Anthropic's AI Research Has Roughly Doubled in Speed Since Late 2025

METR's 11-week assessment estimates Anthropic's internal model capabilities are improving at about 1.9x the 2024-2025 rate, with roughly 80% of that acceleration attributable to AI use rather than more compute. Anthropic held unilateral redaction rights and cut most of the evidence METR found most informative, which METR says forces outsiders to 'rely on our judgment much more than would be ideal.'

Why it matters for you: This is a third party, not a vendor blog post, measuring 'AI accelerating AI R&D' directly — and even it says the public has to take Anthropic's redactions on faith. Expect 'independently audited acceleration' to become a real diligence question for any AI-heavy roadmap.

Meta Pauses a Plan to Train AI on Business Customers' Website Content After Backlash

Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Capability acceleration

How much faster an AI system's measured abilities are improving compared to its historical pace — expressed as a multiple, like '2x,' meaning a year of normal progress now happens in six months. It's the metric at the center of worries about AI improving itself faster than anyone can oversee.

Know-your-customer (KYC) check

The identity verification banks and financial platforms are legally required to run before opening an account, meant to block fraud and money laundering. AI-generated fake IDs are a growing threat to KYC because they can be mass-produced cheaply enough to overwhelm manual review.

ScienceDaily

A Narrow Loophole Might Let a Fast-Spinning Black Hole Split in Two

Physicists modeling black hole thermodynamics found that splitting normally destroys entropy and is forbidden — but extremely fast rotation could open a narrow exception, provided the resulting fragments move apart instead of re-merging. No split has been observed, and the team says they can't yet assign the scenario a probability, but gamma-ray telescopes could one day catch indirect signs of it.

When Every Side Has Its Story: Why Relativism Is the Wrong Answer to Propaganda

Mohsen Askari, an Iranian exile in Istanbul, argues that incompatible accounts of events back home don't mean all narratives are equally true — some conceal harm and some expose it. Drawing on Arendt and Fricker's idea of testimonial injustice, he proposes judging rival narratives by what facts they must deny and whose suffering they erase, rather than shrugging that 'every side has its truth.'

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Memento mori

Latin for 'remember you will die' — a practice, going back to the Stoics, of deliberately keeping mortality in mind as a way to clarify what matters now, rather than as a source of despair.

Testimonial injustice

Philosopher Miranda Fricker's term for when someone's word is given less credibility than it deserves because of prejudice against who they are — a mechanism authoritarian regimes exploit by recasting credible witnesses as 'infiltrators' or 'saboteurs.'
