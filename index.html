Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Usage-based metering

Instead of giving every subscriber the same flat access, a product tracks how much of a shared resource (compute, in ChatGPT's case) each user actually consumes and caps it. Merging two previously separate allowances into one meter, as OpenAI just did, is a quiet way to reallocate cost toward the heaviest users without changing the sticker price.

Bankruptcy estate

When a company files Chapter 11, nearly everything it owns — including, controversially, user data — becomes an asset the court can let it sell to pay creditors, regardless of promises made to users beforehand. Character.AI's chat-data auction is a live test of whether a privacy policy survives its company's bankruptcy.

It Was the Horse, Not the Harvest, That Built the First States

Historian Lhamsuren Munkh-Erdene argues the standard story — that grain surpluses and population pressure produced the state — is a politically loaded myth traced to a 1940s reframing of Chinese frontier claims. He makes the case that it was transportation, chariots, horses, ships, that let warbands mobilize and conquer, putting Eurasia's pastoralist steppe at the center of how states actually formed.

Aeon

Maybe Something Is Simpler Than Nothing

Philosopher Andrew T Brenner challenges Leibniz's assumption that an empty universe would be the simplest possible state. An empty world would still contain truths about what doesn't exist and what could, he argues, so a world explained by one simple thing, God, or a primordial singularity, might actually be simpler overall than true nothingness.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Conquest theory

The rival to the textbook idea that states grew out of farming societies managing grain surpluses. Conquest theory says states formed when mobile warbands, enabled by horses or boats, could mobilize force and subjugate settled farmers — making mobility, not agriculture, the ingredient that made large-scale rule possible.

Non-ontological truth

A true statement that isn't about what exists, like 'dogs could exist' or 'square circles couldn't.' Brenner's essay uses these to argue an empty universe wouldn't actually be as simple as it sounds, since it would still have to contain every one of these truths unexplained.
