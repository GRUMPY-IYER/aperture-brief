Tech 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
National Crime Information Center (NCIC)

A nationwide FBI-run database that local, state and federal police check for warrants, stolen property and criminal histories during ordinary stops and background checks. Because thousands of agencies trust its answers in real time, the system's real vulnerability isn't just data theft — it's anyone quietly altering what a lookup returns.

On-device AI agents

AI assistants built into a phone or computer's operating system that can see and act on whatever is on screen — reading messages, summarizing apps, taking actions on a user's behalf. That convenience is also what worries privacy-focused apps like Signal: an agent with screen access can bypass an app's own encryption simply by looking at what's rendered.

Equality Is Less a Belief Than a Practice We Have to Keep Sustaining

Oxford political theorist Teresa M Bejan traces how "equality" has carried conflicting meanings — balance, indifference, parity — which explains both its historical force and its present-day divisiveness. English Levellers like John Lilburne exploited that ambiguity to demand equal standing through concrete practices like jury service; Bejan argues equality works the same way today, as something people must keep doing together rather than simply affirm.

Why Is There Something Rather Than Nothing? A Simpler Answer Than You'd Expect

Philosopher Andrew T Brenner argues that a world with literally nothing in it would still contain necessary truths — logical, mathematical, moral — so "nothing" was never as simple as it looks. A minimal existing foundation, such as a cosmic singularity or even just humans, could explain those truths more economically, making the actual world arguably the simplest one on offer.

Philosophy 101 for newbies

Today's Concept, Explained Simply
Egalitarianism

The view that people are owed equal standing in some fundamental respect — not necessarily equal outcomes, but equal moral consideration, equal treatment under law, or equal voice in collective decisions. Philosophers disagree sharply about equality of what: wealth, opportunity, capability or respect — which is exactly why the word carries so many conflicting meanings at once.

Principle of sufficient reason

The idea that everything which exists or is true has a reason or cause that explains it — nothing is simply a brute, unexplained fact. It's the engine behind questions like "why is there something rather than nothing": if the principle holds, the universe's existence demands an explanation rather than a shrug.
